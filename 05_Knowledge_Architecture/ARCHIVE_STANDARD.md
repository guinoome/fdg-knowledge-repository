# Purpose

This standard defines how inactive, superseded, obsolete, and historical knowledge assets are managed within the FDG Knowledge Repository.

Archiving preserves organizational history while maintaining the clarity of the active repository.

---

# Core Principle

Inactive knowledge retains organizational value.

Historical engineering reasoning shall be preserved, not discarded.

---

# Objectives

The archive process shall:

- preserve organizational history
- maintain engineering traceability
- reduce repository clutter
- support audits
- protect historical reasoning
- distinguish active knowledge from historical knowledge

---

# Archive Eligibility

A knowledge asset may be archived when it is:

- superseded by an approved version
- no longer operationally active
- retained for legal or audit purposes
- historically significant
- replaced by an evolved engineering approach

Archiving does not imply the knowledge was incorrect.

# Archive Rules

Archived knowledge shall:

remain accessible

retain its original Document ID

retain version history

retain approval history

retain relationships where appropriate

be clearly identified as archived

---

# Superseded Knowledge

When a knowledge asset is superseded:

- the new version becomes the authoritative baseline
- previous versions remain archived
- historical relationships shall be preserved
- references should indicate the current authoritative version

---

# Deletion Policy

Approved organizational knowledge shall not be permanently deleted solely because it is no longer active.

Deletion shall be limited to:

- accidental duplicates
- corrupted assets
- administrative artifacts
- temporary working files with no organizational value

Deletion of governed knowledge requires explicit approval.

---

# Repository Navigation

Archived assets shall not clutter active engineering workflows.

Maps of Content should reference archived assets only when they provide historical context or engineering value.

---

# Archive Quality Checklist

Before archiving, verify:

✓ Successor identified (if applicable).

✓ Historical value assessed.

✓ Relationships preserved.

✓ Metadata updated.

✓ Archive status assigned.

✓ Active references reviewed.

---

# Organizational Principle

Engineering maturity is measured not only by what an organization creates, but also by how it preserves its history.

The archive is an engineering memory, not a storage bin.

---

# Continuous Improvement

Archive practices shall evolve as the repository grows while preserving historical integrity and minimizing disruption to active knowledge.

---

# Governing Principle

Historical engineering knowledge remains part of organizational intelligence even after it is no longer the active baseline.
