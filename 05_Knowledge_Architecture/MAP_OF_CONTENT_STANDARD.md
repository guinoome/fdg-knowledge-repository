# Purpose

This standard defines the role, structure, and governance of Maps of Content (MoCs) within the FDG Knowledge Repository.

Maps of Content provide curated navigation through approved organizational knowledge.

They organize relationships.

They do not duplicate content.

---

# Core Principle

Documents store knowledge.

Maps of Content organize knowledge.

The value of a Map of Content lies in connecting approved knowledge into meaningful engineering context.

---

# Purpose of a Map of Content

A Map of Content (MoC) shall:

- organize related knowledge
- provide navigation
- expose relationships
- improve discoverability
- reduce search effort
- support engineering reasoning

MoCs are knowledge indexes, not knowledge repositories.

---

# Types of Maps of Content

## Repository MoC

Provides entry points into major repository sections.

Example:

Master Index

---

## Domain MoC

Organizes a specific engineering discipline.

Examples:

Mechanical Engineering

Electrical Engineering

Solar Engineering

Hotel Engineering

---

## Topic MoC

Organizes a specialized subject.

Examples:

HVAC

Fire Protection

Project Management

Engineering Calculations

---

## Project MoC

Organizes documentation related to a single project.

Projects reference organizational standards rather than duplicating them.

---

## Process MoC

Organizes engineering workflows.

Examples:

Budget Preparation

Design Review

Incident Investigation

Preventive Maintenance

---

# Structure

Each Map of Content should contain:

Purpose

Scope

Related Standards

Primary Topics

Supporting Documents

Related MoCs

Navigation Links

---

# Responsibilities

Maps of Content shall:

guide navigation

provide context

connect related knowledge

highlight authoritative sources

They shall not become repositories of duplicated information.

---

# Design Rules

Each knowledge asset shall appear in one authoritative location.

Maps of Content shall reference authoritative documents using links.

Cross-linking is preferred over copying.

---

---

# Maintenance

Maps of Content should be reviewed whenever:

new standards are approved

major structural changes occur

new engineering domains are added

obsolete references are identified

Repository growth should improve Maps of Content rather than complicate them.

---

# Quality Criteria

A high-quality Map of Content is:

accurate

current

well-linked

easy to navigate

free from duplication

focused on engineering relationships

---

# Organizational Principle

Maps of Content organize organizational intelligence.

They reduce cognitive effort by making engineering knowledge easy to locate and understand.

---

# Continuous Improvement

Maps of Content shall evolve alongside repository growth while maintaining stable navigation and minimizing disruption.

---

# Governing Principle

Engineering knowledge should be connected through deliberate architecture rather than discovered through accidental search.
