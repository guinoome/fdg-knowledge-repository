# Purpose

This standard defines the engineering evaluation used to determine whether a workflow is suitable for agentic execution.

The objective is to automate only work that improves organizational capability while maintaining engineering governance.

---

# Core Principle

Not every workflow should become autonomous.

Automation shall be justified through engineering evaluation.

The question is not:

"Can this be automated?"

The question is:

"Should this be automated?"

---

# FDG 5R Framework

Every candidate workflow shall be evaluated using the following criteria.

## 1. Repetitive

Question

Does this work occur frequently?

Indicators

- recurring activities
- scheduled operations
- repeated decisions
- recurring reports

Higher repetition increases automation value.

---

## 2. Rules-Based

Question

Can the workflow be described using explicit engineering rules?

Indicators

- documented procedures
- deterministic logic
- defined decision criteria
- engineering standards

Poorly defined rules reduce automation suitability.

---

## 3. Repeatable

Question

Can consistent inputs produce consistent outputs?

Indicators

- standardized process
- stable workflow
- predictable execution
- measurable consistency

Repeatability supports reliable automation.

---

## 4. Reviewable

Question

Can every decision be reviewed and audited?

Indicators

- traceable actions
- recorded reasoning
- reproducible outcomes
- documented assumptions

Engineering governance requires reviewability.

---

## 5. Return on Investment

Question

Does automation provide measurable organizational value?

Possible benefits

- time savings
- quality improvement
- consistency
- risk reduction
- knowledge preservation
- increased organizational capability

Return shall exceed implementation and maintenance effort.

---

# Readiness Assessment

Each criterion shall be evaluated.

High

Medium

Low

Engineering judgment determines readiness.

No workflow shall be automated solely because automation is technically possible.

---

# Engineering Evaluation

Nex shall evaluate:

engineering complexity

↓

operational risk

↓

business value

↓

organizational value

↓

automation readiness

---

# Recommended Outcomes

High Readiness

Suitable for agentic execution.

Proceed with engineering planning.

---

Moderate Readiness

Requires process improvement before automation.

Improve workflow first.

---

Low Readiness

Maintain human execution.

Automation is not currently justified.

---

# Common Reasons for Rejection

Automation should normally be rejected when:

- engineering rules are undefined
- objectives are unclear
- organizational knowledge is incomplete
- reviewability cannot be achieved
- implementation cost exceeds organizational value
- accountability cannot be maintained

---

# Organizational Principle

Automation is not the objective.

Engineering capability is the objective.

Automation is one possible method of achieving it.

---

# Continuous Improvement

Workflows should be periodically reassessed.

Operational experience may improve future readiness.

Engineering maturity increases automation opportunities over time.

---

# Governing Principle

The FDG Ecosystem automates disciplined engineering processes rather than replacing engineering judgment.
