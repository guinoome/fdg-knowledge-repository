# Purpose

This standard defines the architecture, responsibilities, operating principles, and governance of the FDG Optimization Engine.

The Optimization Engine identifies engineering solutions that maximize value while balancing technical performance, safety, lifecycle cost, maintainability, sustainability, operational reliability, and organizational objectives.

Optimization shall always support engineering decision-making and shall never replace engineering judgment.

---

# Mission

Identify the most defensible engineering solution through systematic evaluation of alternatives using validated data, engineering principles, and organizational objectives.

---

# Scope

The Optimization Engine governs optimization activities including:

- Engineering Design
- Equipment Selection
- Energy Systems
- Maintenance Strategies
- Asset Lifecycle
- Operational Performance
- Resource Allocation
- Financial Optimization
- Sustainability
- Risk Reduction

---

# Objectives

The Optimization Engine shall:

- Improve engineering decisions
- Compare multiple engineering alternatives
- Maximize long-term value
- Reduce lifecycle cost
- Improve operational efficiency
- Support evidence-based recommendations
- Preserve engineering transparency

---

# Design Principles

## Principle 1

Optimization shall always evaluate multiple feasible alternatives.

The engine shall never recommend a solution without comparison.

---

## Principle 2

Optimization shall consider the complete engineering lifecycle.

Short-term savings shall never automatically outweigh long-term organizational value.

---

## Principle 3

Optimization shall be objective.

Recommendations shall be supported by measurable engineering criteria rather than subjective preference.

---

## Principle 4

Optimization shall remain transparent.

Every recommendation shall identify why one alternative was preferred over another.

---

## Principle 5

Optimization shall support engineering judgment.

Final engineering decisions remain under qualified human authority.

# Evaluation Criteria

Every alternative shall be evaluated using applicable criteria including:

- Technical Performance
- Safety
- Regulatory Compliance
- Reliability
- Maintainability
- Lifecycle Cost
- Initial Cost
- Operational Cost
- Energy Efficiency
- Sustainability
- Risk
- Scalability

Additional project-specific criteria may be included when justified.

---

# Constraints

The Optimization Engine shall identify constraints before evaluating alternatives.

Examples:

- Budget
- Space
- Schedule
- Available Technology
- Codes and Standards
- Organizational Policies
- Resource Availability
- Client Requirements

Optimization shall never recommend solutions that violate mandatory constraints.

---

# Tradeoff Analysis

Every optimization shall document tradeoffs.

Examples:

Higher Efficiency

↓

Higher Initial Cost

Lower Maintenance

↓

Higher Capital Investment

Lower Energy Consumption

↓

Longer Payback Period

Tradeoffs shall always be visible to decision makers.

---

# Alternative Scoring

Where appropriate, alternatives may be evaluated using weighted engineering criteria.

Typical evaluation categories include:

- Technical
- Financial
- Operational
- Sustainability
- Risk
- Strategic Alignment

Scoring methodologies shall remain transparent and documented.

---

# Recommendation Requirements

Every recommendation shall include:

- Objective

- Risks

# Transparency Requirements

- Evaluation methodology

- Supporting standards
- Engineering judgment applied

Hidden optimization logic is prohibited.
