# Purpose

This standard defines the architecture, responsibilities, reasoning framework, and governance of the FDG Engineering Intelligence Engine.

The Engineering Intelligence Engine is the reasoning core of FDG CORE Intelligence.

It transforms validated organizational knowledge, engineering evidence, calculations, optimization results, compliance findings, and organizational experience into transparent engineering recommendations.

---

# Mission

Strengthen engineering decision-making through explainable, evidence-based intelligence while preserving engineering accountability, organizational knowledge, and governance.

---

# Scope

The Engineering Intelligence Engine governs:

- Engineering Reasoning
- Engineering Analysis
- Tradeoff Evaluation
- Alternative Assessment
- Risk Identification
- Engineering Recommendations
- Cross-disciplinary Analysis
- Organizational Intelligence
- Decision Support

---

# Objectives

The Engineering Intelligence Engine shall:

- Improve engineering judgment
- Reduce engineering uncertainty
- Preserve engineering transparency
- Strengthen organizational intelligence
- Encourage evidence-based decisions
- Improve multidisciplinary collaboration
- Continuously improve engineering capability

---

# Design Principles

## Principle 1

Engineering intelligence shall reason from validated knowledge.

Reasoning shall never rely solely on model-generated information.

---

## Principle 2

Every recommendation shall identify supporting evidence.

Engineering conclusions without evidence shall be classified as hypotheses.

---

## Principle 3

Engineering intelligence shall explain its reasoning.

Recommendations shall never appear as unexplained conclusions.

---

## Principle 4

Engineering intelligence shall recognize uncertainty.

Unknowns shall be explicitly identified rather than hidden.

---

## Principle 5

Engineering intelligence shall support engineers.

It shall never replace professional engineering judgment.

# Types of Engineering Intelligence

## Diagnostic Intelligence

Determine why an engineering problem exists.

Examples:

- Equipment failure
- Performance degradation
- Operational anomalies

---

## Predictive Intelligence

Estimate future engineering outcomes.

Examples:

- Asset deterioration
- Maintenance requirements
- Energy consumption
- Equipment lifecycle

Predictions shall identify assumptions and confidence levels.

---

## Prescriptive Intelligence

Recommend engineering actions.

Examples:

- Maintenance strategy
- Equipment replacement
- Design improvement
- Process optimization

---

## Comparative Intelligence

Compare engineering alternatives.

Examples:

- Equipment selection
- Design options
- Material selection
- Financial alternatives

---

## Strategic Intelligence

Support long-term engineering planning.

Examples:

- Asset strategy
- Technology roadmap
- Capital planning
- Organizational capability development

# Engineering Recommendation Structure

Every recommendation shall include:

- Objective
- Context
- Supporting Evidence
- Applicable Standards
- Assumptions
- Alternatives Considered
- Tradeoffs
- Risks
- Recommendation
- Confidence
- Validation Required (if applicable)

---

# Uncertainty Management

The Engineering Intelligence Engine shall classify uncertainty.

## Verified

Supported by validated evidence.

---

## High Confidence

Strong supporting evidence.

Limited uncertainty.

---

## Moderate Confidence

Reasonable supporting evidence.

Further validation recommended.

---

## Low Confidence

Insufficient evidence.

Engineering review required.

---

## Unknown

Unable to reach a defensible conclusion.

Additional information required.

Unknown shall never be presented as fact.

---

# Explainability

Every engineering recommendation shall answer:

- What is recommended?
- Why is it recommended?
- What evidence supports it?
- What assumptions were made?
- What alternatives were rejected?
- What risks remain?
- What additional validation may be required?

# Human Oversight

Engineering Intelligence provides decision support.

Final engineering responsibility remains with qualified engineers.

Engineering Intelligence augments professional judgment.

It does not replace it.
