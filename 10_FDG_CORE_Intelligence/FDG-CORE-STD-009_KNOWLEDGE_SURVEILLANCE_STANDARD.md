# Purpose

This standard defines the architecture, responsibilities, operating principles, and governance of the FDG Knowledge Surveillance Engine.

The Knowledge Surveillance Engine continuously monitors authoritative engineering knowledge sources for changes that may affect the FDG Knowledge Repository.

Knowledge Surveillance is an intelligence function.

It informs organizational decisions.

It never modifies organizational knowledge autonomously.

---

# Mission

Continuously monitor authoritative engineering knowledge sources to identify new information requiring engineering evaluation while preserving organizational governance and engineering accountability.

---

# Scope

The Knowledge Surveillance Engine governs:

- Standards Monitoring
- Regulatory Monitoring
- Manufacturer Updates
- Engineering Research Monitoring
- Technology Monitoring
- Organizational Knowledge Health
- Knowledge Currency
- Engineering Alerts
- Review Recommendations

---

# Objectives

The Knowledge Surveillance Engine shall:

- Detect relevant engineering changes
- Prevent organizational knowledge obsolescence
- Identify emerging engineering risks
- Recommend knowledge reviews
- Strengthen engineering awareness
- Preserve repository integrity
- Support continuous organizational improvement

---

# Design Principles

## Principle 1

Knowledge Surveillance shall monitor.

It shall never automatically approve.

---

## Principle 2

Knowledge Surveillance shall recommend.

It shall never automatically modify organizational standards.

---

## Principle 3

Only authoritative sources shall be monitored.

Random internet content shall never become an approved surveillance source.

---

## Principle 4

Every detected change shall preserve complete provenance.

All recommendations shall identify:

- Source
- Publication
- Revision
- Date
- Evidence Level

---

## Principle 5

Human engineering governance remains mandatory.

Knowledge Surveillance supports engineering governance.

It does not replace it.

# Impact Assessment

Every detected change shall be evaluated for organizational impact.

Possible classifications include:

## Critical

Immediate engineering review required.

Potential safety, legal, or regulatory implications.

---

## High

Review recommended as soon as practical.

May significantly affect engineering practice.

---

## Moderate

Review during scheduled governance cycle.

---

## Low

Monitor for future evaluation.

Immediate action not required.

---

## Informational

No repository action required.

Awareness only.

---

# Review Recommendation

Every surveillance finding shall generate a review recommendation containing:

- Source
- Summary
- Change Description
- Potential Impact
- Affected Documents
- Evidence Level
- Recommended Action
- Suggested Priority

The recommendation shall not modify repository content.

---

# Repository Protection

Knowledge Surveillance shall never:

- Modify approved standards
- Rewrite organizational knowledge
- Delete repository content
- Override engineering decisions
- Change evidence classifications

Repository modifications require governance approval.

---

# Scheduled Surveillance

Knowledge Surveillance may operate:

- Daily
- Weekly
- Monthly
- Quarterly

depending on source criticality.

Higher-risk knowledge sources should be monitored more frequently.

---

# False Positive Management

The engine shall minimize unnecessary review recommendations.

Every detected change shall be evaluated for:

- Relevance
- Applicability
- Engineering significance
- Organizational impact

Not every external change requires repository modification.

# Human Oversight

Engineering reviewers shall determine:

- Whether the change is applicable
- Whether organizational standards require revision
- Whether implementation is necessary
- Whether further engineering validation is required

Knowledge Surveillance shall never replace engineering governance.

---

# Success Criteria

The Knowledge Surveillance Engine succeeds when:

- Organizational knowledge remains current
- Engineering standards remain relevant
- Repository integrity is preserved
- Important external changes are detected
- Unnecessary repository changes are minimized
- Governance decisions remain evidence-based
