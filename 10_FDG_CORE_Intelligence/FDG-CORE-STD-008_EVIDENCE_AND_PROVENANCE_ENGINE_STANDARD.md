# Purpose

This standard defines the architecture, responsibilities, operating principles, and governance of the FDG Evidence & Provenance Engine.

The Evidence & Provenance Engine establishes trust by ensuring every engineering recommendation, calculation, standard, and organizational knowledge asset is supported by traceable evidence and documented provenance.

Evidence establishes credibility.

Provenance establishes accountability.

---

# Mission

Protect the integrity of the FDG Knowledge Repository by ensuring every accepted engineering statement is supported by verifiable evidence and complete source traceability.

---

# Scope

The Evidence & Provenance Engine governs:

- Evidence Management
- Source Verification
- Knowledge Provenance
- Citation Management
- Engineering References
- Evidence Classification
- Source Authority Assessment
- Knowledge Validation
- Organizational Trust

---

# Objectives

The Evidence & Provenance Engine shall:

- Strengthen organizational trust
- Preserve evidence traceability
- Prevent unsupported knowledge
- Reduce misinformation
- Improve engineering defensibility
- Support engineering governance
- Enable future verification

---

# Design Principles

## Principle 1

Every engineering claim shall identify its supporting evidence.

Unsupported engineering claims shall never become organizational knowledge.

---

## Principle 2

Every source shall be traceable.

Anonymous or unverifiable sources shall not be accepted as authoritative evidence.

---

## Principle 3

Evidence shall be preserved.

The engine shall maintain the provenance of every approved engineering knowledge asset.

---

## Principle 4

Evidence quality shall be evaluated.

Not all evidence carries equal authority.

---

## Principle 5

Evidence shall remain transparent.

Future engineers shall be able to independently verify the basis of every recommendation.

# Evidence Hierarchy

Evidence shall be classified according to organizational authority.

## Level A — Highest Confidence

Authoritative sources.

Examples:

- Laws
- Regulations
- International Standards
- Manufacturer Documentation
- Peer-reviewed Research

---

## Level B — High Confidence

Recognized engineering organizations and independent technical references.

---

## Level C — Moderate Confidence

Validated organizational engineering knowledge.

Examples:

- Approved FDG Standards
- Historical Engineering Records
- Proven Internal Methodologies

---

## Level D — Low Confidence

Professional opinion.

Examples:

- Expert Recommendations
- Collaborator Analysis
- AI-generated Engineering Drafts

Engineering review required.

---

## Level E — Lowest Confidence

Ideas and hypotheses.

Examples:

- Brainstorms
- Concept Discussions
- Experimental Concepts

These shall never become organizational knowledge without validation.

# Provenance Requirements

Every organizational knowledge asset shall preserve:

- Source
- Author
- Document ID
- Version
- Publication Date
- Validation Status
- Evidence Level
- Reviewer
- Approval Status
- Revision History

---

# Source Authority Assessment

The engine shall evaluate:

- Authority
- Authenticity
- Publication Source
- Technical Relevance
- Applicability
- Current Validity

Authority shall not be assumed.

It shall be demonstrated.

---

# Evidence Validation

Evidence shall be evaluated for:

- Authenticity
- Completeness
- Relevance
- Consistency
- Technical Accuracy
- Applicability
- Currency

Evidence failing validation shall be identified before reasoning proceeds.

---

# Evidence Conflicts

When multiple authoritative sources disagree, the engine shall:

- Identify conflicting evidence
- Preserve both positions
- Explain the differences
- Recommend engineering review
- Avoid unsupported conclusions

The engine shall never conceal evidence conflicts.

---

# Provenance Record

Every engineering recommendation shall identify:

- Knowledge Sources Used
- Standards Referenced
- Calculations Referenced
- Organizational Documents Used
- External References
- Supporting Evidence Level
- Confidence Level
