# Service Economics Framework

Service economics evaluates the resources consumed and value required to sustain a service.

Consider direct labor, specialist review, materials, travel, software/tool usage, subcontractors, overhead allocation, risk contingency, rework, warranty/support obligations, and knowledge-development cost where relevant.

Economics should inform packaging and portfolio decisions without overriding mandatory engineering, legal, safety, or quality requirements.
