# Charter and Scope

## Mission

Create a durable FDG organizational capability for converting expertise into controlled, measurable, reusable services.

## Objectives

FSvIS shall:

- establish a canonical service portfolio;
- standardize how services are defined and approved;
- make scope, exclusions, assumptions, dependencies, and deliverables explicit;
- establish repeatable delivery workflows;
- embed evidence and quality controls;
- connect service performance with improvement;
- preserve lessons and reusable components;
- support defensible service costing and pricing intelligence;
- prevent uncontrolled customization from destroying repeatability;
- provide a stable service layer between FDG technical capability and project/client execution.

## In Scope

Service governance, portfolio management, service design, service blueprints, packaging, tiers, delivery standards, service quality, performance intelligence, service economics, service risk, client/service experience, knowledge reuse, continuous improvement, and new-service development.

## Out of Scope

FSvIS shall not become a full CRM, ERP, accounting system, issue tracker, project-management replacement, legal repository, engineering calculation repository, DevOps platform, payment processor, collaboration platform, or general automation engine.

Those functions remain governed by their appropriate FDG intelligence systems.

## Authority Model

FSvIS may define service standards and required evidence. Technical authority remains with the governing engineering or specialist domain. Legal interpretation remains with FLIS and qualified legal authority. Project-specific decisions remain with FPJIS. Security controls remain with FSIS.

## Governance Rule

A service may reference authoritative information from another intelligence system but should not copy and independently maintain the same authoritative knowledge unless an approved controlled snapshot is required.

## Definition of Done for a Mature Service

A service is operationally mature when its purpose, scope, prerequisites, workflow, deliverables, acceptance criteria, risks, responsibilities, evidence, economics, performance measures, and learning loop are sufficiently documented that a competent authorized person can reproduce the intended result without depending on undocumented tribal knowledge.
