# FDG Service Intelligence System (FSvIS)

## Purpose

The FDG Service Intelligence System is the canonical organizational intelligence domain for defining, engineering, packaging, delivering, assuring, measuring, improving, and reusing FDG services.

FSvIS converts FDG capabilities into repeatable service systems. It is not merely a catalogue of offers. It preserves the organizational reasoning required to reproduce a service consistently even when people, software, collaborators, or delivery channels change.

> A service is not mature because FDG can perform it once. It is mature when FDG can define it, reproduce it, verify it, learn from it, and improve it deliberately.

## Core Questions

FSvIS must continuously answer:

- What service is being offered and why does it exist?
- What client or organizational outcome does it create?
- What is included, excluded, assumed, and dependent on other systems?
- What evidence, competencies, resources, workflows, and controls are required?
- What constitutes an acceptable deliverable?
- How is quality verified?
- What risks can cause service failure?
- What does the service cost to deliver and how should its economics be evaluated?
- How is performance measured?
- What knowledge from each delivery should become reusable organizational capability?
- When should a service be improved, standardized, retired, or replaced?

## FDG Engineering Principles

FSvIS follows FDG CORE: Context, Origin, Reasoning, Evidence.

Service decisions must be explainable, traceable, reviewable, reproducible, and defensible. No service shall rely solely on undocumented individual knowledge.

The operating principle is **Capture once, reuse everywhere**.

## System Boundaries

FSvIS owns service intelligence. It does not replace:

- FEIS for engineering-domain knowledge and technical methods.
- FPJIS for project-specific planning, execution, traceability, and project intelligence.
- FBIS or business intelligence domains for enterprise-wide business management.
- FLIS for legal, regulatory, contractual, intellectual-property, and liability intelligence.
- FSIS for security governance and protection controls.
- FCIS/FMCIS for collaboration and multi-collaborator orchestration.
- Platform intelligence systems for platform architecture and technology lifecycle.

FSvIS references these systems rather than duplicating them.

## Service Maturity Model

**S0 — Candidate:** service opportunity identified but not yet defined.

**S1 — Defined:** outcome, scope, boundaries, deliverables, dependencies, and ownership are documented.

**S2 — Repeatable:** delivery workflow, templates, checklists, resource requirements, and acceptance criteria exist.

**S3 — Controlled:** quality controls, evidence requirements, risk controls, change control, and performance measures are operational.

**S4 — Optimized:** service economics, lessons learned, reusable components, benchmarks, and improvement loops are functioning.

**S5 — Scalable:** the service can be delegated, digitally supported, automated where justified, and delivered across projects or collaborators without loss of governance.

## Canonical Service Record

Every mature service should eventually maintain a Service Definition Record containing:

1. Service identity and owner
2. Intended client/user and problem
3. Target outcome and value proposition
4. Scope and exclusions
5. Inputs and prerequisites
6. Applicable requirements and evidence sources
7. Delivery workflow
8. Roles, authority, and competencies
9. Required tools/resources
10. Deliverables
11. Quality and acceptance criteria
12. Risks and controls
13. Dependencies and interfaces
14. Estimated effort and cost model
15. Performance measures
16. Knowledge capture requirements
17. Version and change history
18. Lifecycle status

## Initial Status

This repository is the initial FSvIS baseline. It establishes the architecture and minimum governance needed for future service intelligence. Detailed service catalogues, commercial models, automation, integrations, and domain-specific implementations shall be expanded in controlled future milestones after real workflows are validated.
