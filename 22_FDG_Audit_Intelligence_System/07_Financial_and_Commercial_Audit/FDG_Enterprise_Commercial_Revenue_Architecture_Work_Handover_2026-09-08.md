# FDG Enterprise, Commercial & Revenue Architecture --- Repository Audit Handover

## 1. Purpose

This handover compiles the full architectural discussion that began with
a simple question:

> When FDG project platforms and dashboards go online and begin
> generating income, what type of business will they become?

The discussion established that FDG cannot be classified only as an
engineering SaaS company because its planned capabilities span both:

-   engineering intelligence and engineering services; and
-   business operations, ERP, POS, automation, and digital platforms.

The discussion then exposed a more important architectural issue:
**commercialization creates a legal, accounting, tax, invoicing, IP,
organizational, and enterprise-structure problem that must be resolved
before the first FDG platform begins charging customers.**

This document therefore does **not** declare a new canonical FDG
architecture.

It instructs ChatGPT Work or another authorized collaborator to audit
the actual FDG Knowledge Repository first and determine whether the
required capability already exists, is distributed across existing
systems, or requires a new integrated architecture.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 2. Core Problem Statement

FDG is evolving from founder-executed engineering work toward repeatable
digital capability.

The expected transition is:

``` text
Engineering knowledge / business knowledge
                ↓
Validated FDG workflows
                ↓
Digital platforms and dashboards
                ↓
Automation / intelligence / reusable services
                ↓
Customer-facing products
                ↓
Recurring and transactional revenue
```

This creates a commercial architecture requirement that is broader than
a normal engineering consultancy and broader than a normal ERP/SaaS
company.

The four currently identified revenue families are:

1.  **Engineering Consulting / Automation**
2.  **Engineering Platforms / Subscription**
3.  **Engineering Reports / Audits**
4.  **ERP / POS / Business Platforms**

These are the minimum commercial families to audit. The repository audit
may identify additional legitimate families, but they must not be
invented merely to make the architecture appear comprehensive.

A major conclusion from the discussion is that FDG's engineering and
ERP/business capabilities should not be treated as unrelated businesses
by default.

Example:

Traditional engineering software often stops at the recommendation.

Traditional ERP often begins at the transaction.

FDG has the opportunity to connect:

> **Engineering reasoning → operational decision → business transaction
> → execution → measured result → organizational learning**

This integration is strategically important and should be checked
against the existing responsibilities of \[\[FDG CORE\]\], \[\[FDG
Business Intelligence System\]\], \[\[FDG Engineering Intelligence
System\]\], \[\[FDG Platform Intelligence System\]\], \[\[FDG Project
Intelligence System\]\], automation systems, and governance systems.

# 5. CORE Integrated Engineering, FDG, and Future Enterprises

## Critical architectural concern

**CORE Integrated Engineering, FDG, and future enterprises must not
become three competing architectural concepts.**

The audit must establish their exact relationship and separation of
responsibilities.

Do not begin by assuming any of the following:

``` text
CORE Integrated Engineering → FDG → Future Enterprises
```

or:

``` text
FDG → CORE Integrated Engineering → Future Enterprises
```

or any other hierarchy.

The correct relationship must be derived from:

1.  actual repository architecture;
2.  current legal/commercial reality;
3.  existing brand and operating documents;
4.  existing governance;
5.  future commercial requirements;
6.  liability and professional-accountability boundaries;
7.  taxation and registration requirements;
8.  IP ownership;
9.  investment/ownership requirements; and
10. long-term organizational scalability.

## Existing evidence to verify

Existing FDG materials have previously described **CORE Integrated
Engineering** as a future parent/primary brand and **FDG Solar Visayas**
as an operating division. Other FDG materials describe platforms as
operating under the FDG Ecosystem.

This is evidence of architectural intent, but **not sufficient evidence
to declare the final enterprise hierarchy**.

The audit must reconcile these representations with the current FDG
Knowledge Repository.

# 9. BIR / Invoice / Receipt / Registration Concern

The trigger for this audit is not merely branding.

The concern is avoiding a future conflict where:

-   the platform sells one type of service;
-   the registered business activity describes another;
-   contracts use inconsistent entities;
-   invoices use inconsistent classifications;
-   payment accounts do not match the seller;
-   accounting recognizes revenue differently from the commercial
    product;
-   IP ownership is unclear;
-   the customer-facing brand differs from the legal seller without
    adequate disclosure.

The audit must therefore verify alignment across the relevant Philippine
legal/commercial layers, including where applicable:

``` text

Invoices
          ↕

          ↕
```

# 15. Shared Platform vs. Domain Logic

Commercialization must preserve the existing architectural principle
that shared CORE services should not become a giant monolith.

A useful responsibility model to verify is:

``` text
Shared platform primitives
    identity
    organizations / tenants
    evidence
    data
    documents
    permissions
    billing primitives
    auditability
    common integrations
    shared reporting infrastructure

Domain modules
    retain domain-specific engineering/business logic
```

Example:

-   T&C knows how to commission a pump.
-   Energy Audit knows how to establish an energy baseline.
-   ERP knows how to execute inventory/procurement/business
    transactions.
-   Shared CORE capabilities know how these activities reference common
    identity, assets, evidence, permissions, transactions, and
    provenance.

The commercial layer should orchestrate existing ownership rather than
absorb all domain logic.
