> **Primary mandate:** Dashboard / Platform = Product Experience + Marketing Asset + Operational Tool + Business Growth Instrument.

This mandate extends existing FPIS standards and supersedes in part any interpretation that a technically correct generic dashboard is an acceptable final experience. Preserve earlier knowledge and proven workflows; patch forward rather than destructively rebuilding.

## 1. Governing outcome

FDG builds premium operating experiences that turn data into understanding, understanding into decisions, decisions into action, and action into measurable engineering and business value.

**Premium Experience + Real Workflow + Trust + Evidence + Business Value**

A completed experience lets the user operate it, the client understand it, the owner see value, a prospect become interested, a demonstration create demand, and future agents continue without reconstructing completed work.

## 2. Four simultaneous responsibilities

Every platform is:

1. **Product experience:** intentional, fast, domain-native, responsive, accessible, trustworthy, and difficult to misuse.
2. **Marketing asset:** explains the problem, value, risk reduction, differentiation, proof, and next action without a long sales explanation.
3. **Operational tool:** working navigation, input, validation, persistence, evidence, feedback, permissions, reporting, and recovery.
4. **Business-growth instrument:** supports attraction, conversion, adoption, retention, referral, expansion, and recurring value.

Visual polish never compensates for fake workflow. Technical correctness never excuses a generic or commercially weak experience.

## 3. Premium means intentional

Premium means obvious value, strong hierarchy and typography, controlled density, refined feedback, domain-specific graphics, deliberate mobile behavior, fast task completion, realistic data, useful visualization, credible evidence, professional output, and thoughtful empty/loading/error/offline/success states.

It does not mean excessive motion, decorative gradients, generic SaaS language, repetitive cards, or heavy interfaces.

The quality benchmark is the intentionality, perceived completeness, product identity, interaction quality, emotional engagement, and conversion awareness demonstrated by [ML Digital Event Platform](https://mlprinting.vercel.app/), not its invitation-specific visual style.

## 4. Platform families

### FDG Engineering Platform

Engineering experiences cover analysis, inspection, testing, commissioning, maintenance, evidence, findings, deterministic recommendations, approval, signatures, revisions, offline field capture, and reporting. Reusable identity, measurement, evidence, recommendation, and reporting primitives must not be rebuilt for each asset. Fire Pump PM is a domain template under the reusable preventive-maintenance and evidence-capture architecture, not a separate platform.

### FDG Business Platform

Business experiences cover fuel, restaurants, sari-sari retail, tire service, bookings, trucking/logistics, bakery, bus, taxi, car rental, sales, inventory, staff, customers, branches, fleet, deliveries, profitability, cash control, and reporting. Each vertical retains domain-native language and workflow.

Architecture must scale without redesign:

**Single Site → Multiple Branches → Regional Network → Enterprise Portfolio**

CAPEX commercial evaluation belongs primarily to the Business Platform / FBPOIS, with FEIS providing the traceable engineering basis where required.

- [[08_FEIS_Engineering_Intelligence_Systems/FEIP-STD-001_PLATFORM_ARCHITECTURE_AND_MODULE_DESIGN_STANDARD|FEIS]] owns engineering meaning and workflow.

FPIS must not redefine domain truth merely because it displays it.

## 6. Mobile web is primary

Priority is mobile web / PWA, tablet, desktop browser, then large-display command views when justified. Native apps follow only when deeper device integration has measurable value.

Use **progressive rearrangement, not progressive information loss**:

- usable touch targets, one-hand actions, phone-appropriate forms, and suitable input keyboards;
- camera and QR paths where the workflow requires them;
- progressive disclosure, bottom sheets/tabs, drill-down, and intelligent table transformation;
- continuous local save with visible Local, Offline, Waiting, Syncing, Synchronized, Failed, and Review Required states;
- optimized imagery, limited initial JavaScript, deferred 3D, low-bandwidth support, and reduced motion.

Never shrink a desktop dashboard until it merely fits a phone.

## 7. Connected public and operational experience

Public/prospect surfaces attract, explain, prove, differentiate, build trust, and convert with domain visuals, outcome-led copy, workflow demonstrations, evidence, use cases, before/after states, and a clear next action.

Logged-in surfaces execute work, capture data, monitor, decide, preserve evidence, collaborate, approve, report, and retain history. Marketing hierarchy may clarify value but may not interrupt the operational task.

**Attract → Understand → Explain → Prove → Decide → Act → Measure → Retain → Grow**

## 8. Domain-native graphics

Graphics are product infrastructure. Use equipment diagrams, annotated evidence, performance curves, acceptance zones, topology, flows, heatmaps, findings, timelines, branch maps, service bays, booking timelines, fleet routes, production flows, wet-stock movement, profitability drivers, and demand maps where they clarify meaning.

Fuel experience communicates forecourt, pumps, tanks, wet stock, deliveries, shift closeout, safety, sales, and network visibility.

Use motion and 3D only when they improve understanding, differentiation, or conversion. Generated geometry and extracted data remain Generated or Pending Verification until human review.

## 9. Protected truth and semantics

Marketing may change hierarchy, storytelling, onboarding, CTA placement, and executive summary. It must not change measurements, limits, safety states, provenance, calculations, approvals, compliance meaning, or audit history.

Use **configurable experience, not configurable engineering truth**.

- Green — Verified / Normal / Accepted
- Amber — Attention / Deviation / Pending
- Red — Failure / Critical
- Blue — Measured / Actual data
- Gray — Reference / Existing state
- Outline — Design / Expected state
- Pulse — Live / Changing state
- Dashed — Proposed / Simulated state

Never rely on color alone.

## 10. Intelligence is assistance, not authority

Future OCR, photo understanding, recognition, anomaly detection, spatial inference, prediction, query, and report drafting require provenance, review, correction, and approval.

Evidence pipeline:

Capture → Classify → Recognize → Extract Proposed Data → User Confirms → Build Relationships → Generate View → User Reviews → Approved Context

Deterministic recommendation pipeline:

Observation + Reading + Rule + Evidence → Finding → Severity → Action → Priority / Due Date → Retest

## 11. Storage, offline, and synchronization

Client deployment may use local storage, local-first cache, client-owned storage, Google Drive/Sheets, a platform database, or an approved hybrid. Gmail sign-in may make Drive/Sheets the default client-facing option where appropriate, but complex relational, high-volume, binary, audit, and synchronization data needs fit-for-purpose storage.

Distinguish user-visible records, evidence/files, transactions, synchronization metadata, and audit history. Never allow silent data loss. Offline field work must support resume, autosave, deferred synchronization, retry, and visible state. This mandate does not select a provider or authorize integration.

## 12. Identity, signature, attribution, and white label

Authenticated operator identity and displayed report date remain distinct from created, updated, submitted, signed, and synchronized timestamps. Signature capture is an acknowledgement with identity, role, organization, time, project, revision, technician, text, and sync state; it is not called a qualified signature without the required infrastructure.

Provider attribution and experience configuration must be centralized. Tenant profiles may alter logo, typography, imagery, navigation, density, and terminology, but protected semantics remain fixed and per-client forks are avoided.

## 13. High-fidelity gate

Major experiences require an accepted visual target: high-fidelity mockup, generated reference, interactive prototype, motion storyboard, mobile screen set, desktop presentation, or public landing surface.

Implementation must be compared with it. **Excellent concept → basic coded dashboard** is an unfinished release gap, not completion.

Capture and review mobile home, primary workflow, data entry, success state, desktop overview, core domain visualization, and public experience. Ask whether the domain and value are obvious in 15–30 seconds and visibly stronger than a generic admin template.

## 14. Functional, accessibility, and performance gates

Verify navigation, input, validation, save, edit/delete where permitted, offline behavior, sync state, loading, error, retry, empty state, permission state, report/export, touch behavior, refresh/recovery, and autosave. Demo-only behavior must be labeled.

Require contrast, readable type, semantic structure, focus states, keyboard access, usable targets, reduced motion, meaningful errors, zoom, and non-color cues.

Premium must remain fast: optimize images, lazy-load noncritical media, prevent layout shift, minimize JavaScript, defer expensive spatial work, cache deliberately, and preserve low-bandwidth operation.

## 15. Outcome language and honest proof

Prefer credible outcomes over “Dashboard,” “Smart Solution,” or “Advanced Analytics.” Show an immediate advantage over spreadsheets, paper, or generic POS/admin software: explainable movement, visible exception, verified closeout, evidence-linked decision, or multi-branch insight.

Never invent live integrations, sensor feeds, compliance, approval, predictive accuracy, AI capability, or completed automation. Label Concept, Prototype, Simulated Data, Planned Capability, Beta, and Pending Verification.

## 16. Rejected patterns

Reject generic sidebars with random KPIs, repetitive card walls, decorative stock charts, placeholder tables, default component-library appearance, desktop squeezed onto mobile, tiny tables, hidden critical information, hover-only action, public pages without proof/CTA, opaque AI conclusions, unsupported claims, missing evidence, and visually impressive but misleading status.

## 17. Additive release and no-rework rule

v0.1 functional core → v0.1.x experience/workflow patches → v0.2 intelligence/automation → v0.x hardening → v1 production

Before changing work, inspect the current handover, implementation, references, governance, and Git state. Determine whether it is incomplete, incorrect, superseded, or intentionally provisional. Extend or patch where possible; do not rebuild because another agent prefers a different implementation.

## 18. Handover continuity

Before agent switch, likely quota/context interruption, or ending incomplete work, refresh a handover with completed features/files/routes/data/design/tests/deployment, decisions and tradeoffs, current branch/environment, working/partial/broken behavior, blockers/assumptions/debt, exact ordered next actions, evidence paths, and repository updates.

Use `HANDOVER_[Platform]_[Module]_[YYYY-MM-DD_HHMM].md` for historical records and maintain a current pointer. Never delete historical handovers.

## 19. Start and completion procedure

Start: identify platform family → read this mandate → read current handover/specification → inspect implementation/render/reference → select the smallest forward patch → execute.

Finish: run functional checks → review mobile and desktop → capture and compare screenshots → fix regressions → record gaps → update repository decisions → refresh handover.

Before every major decision ask whether it advances premium experience, functionality, credibility, attraction/conversion/retention/expansion, protected truth, and reuse. If not, reconsider it.
