## 2. Mandatory architecture

Preserve this structure exactly:

**FEXIS discovers. Filmmaking Intelligence understands. Creative Studio produces. FBIS measures commercial value.**

Expanded responsibilities:
- **FEXIS:** discovers external sources, creators, methods, trends, platform changes and signals. Preserve provenance and source freshness.
- **FDG Filmmaking Intelligence (FFI):** understands, compares, classifies, validates and converts learning into reusable methods, standards, prompts, production workflows and acceptance criteria.
- **FDG Creative Studio:** produces approved assets through a provider-neutral creation experience and replaceable adapters.
- **FBIS:** defines audience, offer, campaign and commercial intent; measures qualified inquiries, pipeline, conversion, collected revenue and acquisition economics.

Supporting authorities:
- **FEIS:** technical/engineering facts, calculations, simulations and validation.
- **FWAIS:** approved automation, orchestration, scheduling and distribution.
- **FLIS / FSIS:** licensing, copyright, privacy, consent, data handling and security.
- **FAIS:** evidence, audit trail, approvals and control effectiveness.
- **FMCIS:** collaborator roles, handoffs, coordination and acceptance.
- **FPJIS:** scoped work, implementation, test and delivery records.
- **FDG CORE / Knowledge Management / Knowledge Architecture:** organizational knowledge governance and promotion.

Do not create another top-level filmmaking or AI-video intelligence system. Extend the current FPIS domain master.

## 4. Course review work package

### A. Public outline review

Start with FFI-SRC-001. Check whether the current public outline has changed and record the date. It currently advertises:
- AI filmmaking tools, VFX, AI editing, AI clones and cinematic video generation.
- Short-form, long-form YouTube, ads and short films.
- AI image and graphic generation.
- AI thumbnails.
- Content planning, niche/competitor research, calendars and scripting.
- AI background/studio replacement.
- Editing, captions, podcast editing and audio enhancement.
- A short-form course bonus covering platform algorithms, hooks, formats, growth and monetization.

These are publisher-stated curriculum topics, not independently verified capabilities or guaranteed outcomes.

### B. Enrolled lessons, if legitimately accessible

For each lesson capture exact module and lesson name, source location, date, tools/models/versions, prompts/settings, input assets, steps, output, failure cases, costs, time, licensing, privacy, repeatability and evidence. Do not copy or redistribute copyrighted course materials into the repository. Store FDG's own concise notes, findings and original methods with references to the source.

If no collaborator has access, stop at the public-source review and request authorized notes/transcripts from the founder. Never bypass access controls or gated course material.

### C. Compare and classify

For each technique, select one:
- **REUSE** — existing FDG method already covers it.
- **EXTEND** — add a missing capability or acceptance criterion to an existing method.
- **MERGE** — reconcile overlapping methods with an explicit decision.
- **REPLACE** — only with evidence that the existing method is materially inferior and after approval.
- **NEW** — only when a genuine domain gap exists.

Mark every conclusion as observed fact, publisher claim, analyst interpretation, hypothesis, calculated result or FDG test result.

## 5. Cross-functional collaborator assignments

### FEXIS collaborator — discovery and source integrity
- Confirm canonical course page and any changed URLs or public curriculum.
- Identify official documentation for tools and platforms mentioned in lessons.
- Find primary sources for material claims; record dates and versions.
- Suggest a source monitoring and update model with duplicate detection and change history.
- Do not assume the FEXIS external-intelligence charter means the video-monitoring pipeline already exists.

### Filmmaking / creative specialist — craft and production method
- Assess storytelling, pacing, hooks, payoff, scene continuity, shot design, visual direction, sound, captions and editability.
- Compare course methods to the six existing YouTube prompts and FDG Production Playbook.
- Recommend which techniques should become reusable FDG templates, and which should remain situational.
- Identify the smallest production exercise that tests a method.

### Creative Studio / toolchain collaborator — production feasibility
- Map each useful task to provider-neutral capabilities: image generation, video generation, voice, editing, compositing, storyboarding, asset management and export.
- Review Huashu Motion and Visual Craft references before suggesting another tool.
- Compare quality, controllability, consistency, editable source, export, latency, retries, cost and portability.
- Never make a provider the architecture owner; do not install tools or incur costs without authorization.

### FBIS collaborator — commercial value
- Define which FDG offer/product/service should be supported by the pilot.
- Specify target audience, pain point, value proposition, CTA and conversion path.
- Define metrics beyond views: qualified inquiries, lead-to-opportunity conversion, quotation, paid conversion and collected revenue.
- Distinguish reach/engagement from actual commercial results.
- Review the existing FBIS content engine before suggesting a new funnel.

### FEIS collaborator — engineering truth
- For engineering explainers or product demonstrations, identify claims, diagrams, geometry, measurements and operating states requiring engineering review.
- Establish source evidence and acceptance tests for every technical assertion.
- Prevent generated visuals from fabricating equipment configuration, safety conditions or performance data.
- Recommend where simulation/illustration must be labelled as such.

### FWAIS collaborator — automation and distribution
- Identify repeatable tasks that could be automated after manual workflow validation.
- Assess APIs, platform rules, permissions, quotas, rate limits, retry/recovery, idempotency, monitoring and rollback.
- Separate production automation from public posting. Publication remains approval-gated.
- No scraping or unauthorized account access; no claim of operational monitoring without verified scheduled execution and logs.

### FLIS / FSIS collaborator — rights and security
- Review source footage, music, fonts, likenesses/voice cloning, generated assets, course content, model terms and commercial usage.
- Check privacy and confidential information in prompts, uploads and external APIs.
- Define retention, deletion, consent, data egress and secret handling.
- Do not upload course material or sensitive FDG/customer data to third-party services without authorization.

### FAIS / FMCIS / FPJIS collaborator — governance and execution
- Define review evidence, approvers, work packages, status, acceptance and change history.
- Ensure proposed standards remain proposals until reviewed and accepted.
- Keep a record of disagreements, unresolved assumptions and rejected methods.
- Create follow-on implementation tickets only after the research decision is made.

## 6. Required collaborator response format

Every collaborator returns one concise, evidence-backed review using this structure:

1. **Role / scope reviewed**
2. **Sources inspected** — exact URLs, repository paths, versions, dates, lesson references.
3. **What is genuinely useful** — with evidence.
4. **What FDG already has** — exact wikilinks and overlap.
5. **Gaps or contradictions** — why current material is insufficient.
6. **Recommended disposition** — REUSE / EXTEND / MERGE / REPLACE / NEW.
7. **Proposed repository changes** — exact file, section and wikilinks; additive changes only.
8. **Testable experiment** — inputs, steps, cost/time, output and acceptance criteria.
9. **Risks / dependencies / unknowns**
10. **Status** — documented, source-reviewed, prototyped, runtime-verified, pilot-accepted or operational.

Do not submit generic “AI can do X” lists. Show what changes in FDG's workflow, evidence quality, cost, time, quality or commercial outcome.

## 7. First integrated FDG video workflow — bounded pilot

After initial reviews, run one end-to-end workflow. Recommended starting format: a 45–60 second FDG product or engineering explainer based on one real, already implemented capability.

Workflow:
1. FBIS supplies audience, business objective, offer and CTA.
2. FEXIS supplies relevant external research and source references.
3. FFI writes the creative brief, script, visual bible, storyboard and shot list.
4. FEIS or the responsible owner validates all technical claims.
5. Creative Studio produces and edits the assets with replaceable tools.
6. FLIS / FSIS review rights, consent, privacy and data handling.
7. Creative and domain reviewers approve the final cut.
8. FWAIS may prepare a distribution package; public release requires explicit approval.
9. FBIS records performance and commercial outcomes.
10. FFI captures lessons, failed attempts and validated methods into the repository.

Acceptance criteria:
- One approved brief and measurable audience objective.
- Script has a clear promise, meaningful payoff and no unsupported claim.
- Visual bible and editable shot list preserve continuity.
- Technical claims have traceable source evidence and accountable review.
- Assets have documented provenance and rights status.
- Final render passes audio, caption, aspect ratio, legibility and mobile QA.
- CTA resolves correctly and analytics are configured.
- Production time, retries, editing effort and actual cost are recorded.
- Performance report distinguishes views/engagement from qualified leads and revenue.
- Repository captures the complete evidence chain and lessons learned.

No specific provider, paid subscription, course purchase or public release is authorized by this handover.

## 8. Research decision gates

- **Gate 1 — Source review:** public outline and source integrity complete.
- **Gate 2 — Lesson review:** actual enrolled lessons reviewed where authorized; otherwise explicitly pending.
- **Gate 3 — Gap analysis:** compare with existing FDG material and propose changes.
- **Gate 4 — Experiment:** test one high-value method with baseline and acceptance criteria.
- **Gate 5 — Promotion:** only validated methods become reusable FDG standards.
- **Gate 6 — Production pilot:** complete end-to-end workflow and measure quality, cost, time and commercial value.

Do not skip from marketing claims to adoption.

## 9. Canonical knowledge flow

**FEXIS discovers → Filmmaking Intelligence understands → Creative Studio produces → FBIS measures commercial value.**

The loop closes when measured production outcomes return to Filmmaking Intelligence as evidence-backed learning. FEXIS continues to monitor external changes; it does not redefine FDG's validated production truth.
