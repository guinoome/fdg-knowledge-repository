# FDG Filmmaking Intelligence — Production Playbook

> [[FDG Ecosystem|FDG Ecosystem]] → [[17_FDG_Platform_Intelligence_System/00_FPI_Home|FPIS]] → [[17_FDG_Platform_Intelligence_System/07_Platform_Experience_Design_Intelligence/FDG_Filmmaking_Intelligence_Master_Index|Filmmaking Intelligence Master]] → this document.

## 1. Purpose

Provide a repeatable, provider-neutral production workflow that converts a real FDG objective into an approved, editable, measurable film or video deliverable.

This playbook is a starting operating model. It is not a claim that a production team, automated pipeline, or particular provider is already operational.

## 2. Select the production format

| Format | Primary job | Typical output | Critical acceptance |
|---|---|---|---|
| Short-form Reel / Short | Earn attention and deliver one useful idea | Vertical short video | Hook clarity, retention, payoff, mobile readability |
| Long-form YouTube | Explain, teach or document a topic | Structured episode | Narrative coherence, depth, pacing, factual accuracy |
| Product launch film | Communicate a real product value proposition | Cinematic product film | Real feature fidelity, clear differentiation, truthful CTA |
| Engineering explainer | Explain a system, process or technical finding | Animated/annotated explainer | Engineer-verified geometry, data and claims |
| Educational lesson | Improve understanding or skill | Lesson with visual sequence and assessment | Learning objective, technical correctness, comprehension |
| Case study / testimonial | Establish credible proof | Evidence-led story | Consent, source evidence, representativeness and claim approval |
| Campaign creative | Support an approved campaign | Multi-channel asset set | Audience fit, offer consistency, conversion tracking |

## 3. Creative brief

Before scripting, define:
- objective and intended action;
- target audience and prior knowledge;
- platform, aspect ratio, duration and language;
- one-sentence promise;
- problem/tension and payoff;
- verified facts, source evidence and prohibited claims;
- tone and visual direction;
- available original assets and rights;
- CTA and destination;
- success metric and baseline;
- approvers and release constraints.

Do not start with a prompt. Start with the intended audience outcome and the evidence available.

## 4. Story and script

A useful story structure:

1. **Opening:** establish a relevant question, tension, surprising fact or visual event.
2. **Promise:** make clear what the viewer will understand or receive.
3. **Development:** sequence evidence, demonstration or narrative beats.
4. **Payoff:** resolve the opening promise; do not withhold essential information artificially.
5. **Action:** one relevant next step, when appropriate.

Hook patterns are options, not mandatory formulas:
- pattern interrupt;
- curiosity gap;
- contrarian claim, only when substantiated;
- before/after or problem/result;
- demonstration-first;
- unresolved visual question;
- looped ending that makes the opening newly meaningful.

Retention design must not use fabricated stakes, false urgency, misleading thumbnails, deceptive edits or unsupported controversy.

## 5. Visual bible and continuity

For multi-shot or generated scenes, establish a compact visual bible:
- subject/product/character identity and reference images;
- location and environment;
- dimensions, geometry, labels and component IDs where factual;
- color, materials, wardrobe and props;
- lighting and time-of-day;
- lens/framing/movement direction;
- permitted state changes;
- continuity invariants and known exclusions.

Use reference-led workflows when useful. A scene must not silently change the identity, physical configuration, product features or technical state of its subject.

For engineering films, separate technical source data from the creative rendering layer. Use FEIS-approved geometry, diagrams, calculations or simulation outputs; generative visuals are illustrative unless independently validated.

## 6. Storyboard and shot list

Every shot should specify:
- shot/scene ID and time range;
- narrative purpose;
- subject/action/state;
- shot size, angle, composition and camera movement;
- location, lighting and visual treatment;
- dialogue/narration/on-screen text;
- sound/music intent;
- transition in/out;
- asset/source references;
- continuity notes;
- evidence class;
- acceptance criterion.

Create an editable source document, not only screenshots or a flattened prompt.

## 7. Production and editing

Possible workflow:
1. Generate or capture source assets with approved tools.
2. Select takes against the storyboard rather than accepting first outputs.
3. Maintain scene/asset identifiers and version history.
4. Assemble an edit with purposeful pacing and readable transitions.
5. Design sound and music with explicit licensing checks.
6. Add accurate captions and mobile-safe text.
7. Produce the target aspect ratios and export settings.
8. Preserve editable project files and final render.

Tool choices are task-specific. [[17_FDG_Platform_Intelligence_System/07_Platform_Experience_Design_Intelligence/09_Huashu_Motion_Cinematic_Product_Film_Adapter_2026-10-23|Huashu Motion]] is an optional source-reviewed code-driven adapter, not a mandatory tool. Other providers remain replaceable.

## 8. Quality gates

**Creative review**
- Does the opening earn attention without deception?
- Is the story understandable on first viewing?
- Does each shot add information or emotion?
- Are pacing, continuity, composition and sound intentional?
- Is the ending/payoff satisfying?
- Are captions, contrast and mobile readability adequate?

**Domain review**
- Are facts, numbers, product functions and technical diagrams correct?
- Are calculations traceable and independently reproducible?
- Are simulated and illustrative content clearly labeled?
- Are limitations and uncertainty represented?
- Has the accountable domain owner approved the claim?

**Rights / privacy / security review**
- Are source footage, music, fonts, stock, likenesses and generated assets authorized?
- Are releases and consent obtained where needed?
- Is confidential client/site data excluded or specifically authorized?
- Are provider terms and data-egress constraints satisfied?

**Commercial review**
- Is the offer current and deliverable?
- Is the CTA functional and measured?
- Are testimonials and performance claims evidenced?
- Does publication support a real business objective?

## 9. Release and measurement

Before release, record:
- final asset and version;
- approvers and dates;
- publication channel and destination;
- caption, title, thumbnail and CTA;
- source/rights/provenance record;
- domain and legal/security approvals where required;
- release owner and rollback/takedown route.

Measure by objective. Separate:
- impressions/reach;
- views and view-through;
- average watch time / retention curve;
- completion and replay;
- saves/shares/comments;
- link clicks and qualified inquiries;
- lead-to-opportunity progression;
- paid conversion and collected revenue;
- production cost, retries and editor hours.

Views and followers are not revenue. A demo is not customer acceptance. A quotation is not payment.

## 10. Reusable short-form prompt patterns

The repository already contains a YouTube prompt collection with six useful starting patterns:
1. Pattern Interrupt Hook.
2. Retain & Reward script (curiosity gap and three value points).
3. Contrarian Take.
4. Infinite Loop Closer.
5. Visual Storyboarder (three-second scene intervals).
6. Viral Title & Thumbnail Duo.

Source: [[Projects/Active/AI/Youtube Prompt.txt|YouTube Prompt Collection]].

These prompts are raw references, not proven formulas. Adapt them to the brief, test them, remove misleading clickbait and record measured results before promoting any pattern.

## 12. First bounded pilot recommendation

Produce one 45–60 second original FDG product/engineering explainer using an existing real capability and approved evidence.

Candidate: demonstrate one real workflow from user problem → actual FDG product interaction → verified outcome → next action. If the product interaction is simulated, label it clearly. Do not claim a feature exists until it is implemented and tested.

Acceptance:
- one approved brief;
- script with source references;
- visual bible and editable shot list;
- rights-cleared assets;
- domain-reviewed claims;
- captioned final render;
- creative and mobile QA;
- functional CTA;
- measurement plan and post-release result record.

No provider, spend or public release is authorized by this playbook alone.
