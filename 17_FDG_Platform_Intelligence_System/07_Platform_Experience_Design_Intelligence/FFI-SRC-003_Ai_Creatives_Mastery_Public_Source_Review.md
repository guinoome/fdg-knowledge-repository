# FFI-SRC-003 — AI Creatives Mastery: Public Source Review

> [[FDG Ecosystem|FDG Ecosystem]] → [[17_FDG_Platform_Intelligence_System/07_Platform_Experience_Design_Intelligence/FDG_Filmmaking_Intelligence_Master_Index|FDG Filmmaking Intelligence]] → this review.

## 1. Scope and evidence boundary

Reviewed the publicly accessible landing page at [aivideomastery.site](https://aivideomastery.site/) on 2026-11-10. The page brands the offer **AI Creatives Mastery** and promotes a “Zero to Hero: AI Video Course”.

This is a public-page review only. No paid enrollment, member classroom, lesson video, downloadable resource, AI tool, community discussion or production workflow was accessed or tested. All course contents, prices, member counts, reviews, income opportunities and performance claims below are publisher statements unless explicitly identified as FDG analysis.

The supplied URL contains advertising/referral tracking parameters. The canonical source URL is https://aivideomastery.site/.

## 2. Publicly advertised curriculum

The landing page advertises seven modules:

1. **Storytelling, Human Psychology & Persuasion** — framing creative work around audience attention, motivation and persuasion.
2. **Scripting & Creative Direction** — turning an idea into a structured script and directing intent.
3. **Visual Storytelling & Shot Design** — choosing shots and visual sequences to communicate the story.
4. **Visual Development & Continuity** — developing a coherent visual treatment and maintaining consistency.
5. **AI Video Generation & Motion Direction** — generating moving imagery and directing motion.
6. **Editing, Sound Design & Pacing** — assembling clips, shaping rhythm and using sound deliberately.
7. **Distribution, Measurement & Iteration** — distributing work, reading performance and improving subsequent versions.

Other publicly advertised resources:
- **AI Studio Tools:** an AI Video Editor described as trimming mistakes, captioning and assembling clips; an AI Scriptwriter that turns an idea into episodes/scenes/prompts; Brand Analysis from an uploaded logo; a Prompt Builder; Hook and CTA generators; reference sheets and design styles.
- **Creator Assets & Guides:** 60+ cheatsheets, cinematic prompt packs, motion-design mini-courses and bonus ebooks/guides.
- **Commercial pathways:** product ads/commercial reels for business owners; freelance AI video services; faceless creator/affiliate content; paid ads for ecommerce brands.
- **Advertised monetization channels:** TikTok/Shopee affiliate commissions, freelance projects, faceless social-channel monetization and paid-ad creative services.

The page claims smartphone/basic-laptop accessibility, lifetime access, future updates and community support. These are publisher claims and have not been tested.

## 4. Useful ideas for FDG

### 4.1 Seven-stage craft sequence

The most useful structural idea is the explicit sequence from persuasion and creative direction through shot design, continuity, motion, editing/sound and measured iteration. It reinforces the existing FDG workflow rather than requiring a new architecture.

**Disposition: REUSE + EXTEND.** Reuse the existing FFI Production Playbook. Extend its production brief and acceptance criteria with an explicit audience-psychology hypothesis and a continuity review across the storyboard, generated takes and final edit.

### 4.2 Continuity as a first-class production control

The dedicated visual-development/continuity module supports treating identity, product configuration, wardrobe, environment, lighting, camera direction and permitted state changes as controlled invariants—not as prompt wording alone.

**Disposition: EXTEND.** Add a shot-to-shot continuity matrix to the pilot package and evaluate continuity against approved reference assets. For engineering/product work, FEIS-approved geometry and operating states remain authoritative.

### 4.3 Brand analysis and reusable creative direction

The advertised logo-to-brand-report feature suggests a repeatable brand-intake artifact: color, typography, visual style and usage constraints.

**Disposition: EXTEND.** Define a provider-neutral FDG Brand Visual Profile input/output contract. It must distinguish extracted observations from approved brand rules and retain human review. Do not adopt or reproduce the advertised tool without testing.

### 4.4 Prompt construction should be downstream of production intent

The advertised scriptwriter, prompt builder, hook and CTA generators are useful as interface patterns, but they should consume an approved brief, visual bible and shot specification. They must not replace those artifacts or generate unsupported claims.

**Disposition: REUSE / EXTEND.** Build on existing FFI brief, script, visual bible and shot-list structures; do not create disconnected prompt silos.

### 4.5 Editing, sound and pacing are part of the deliverable

The course gives explicit attention to sound design and pacing. This reinforces that generation alone is not a finished video. Editing, audio, captions, mobile legibility, versioning and export QA remain required.

**Disposition: REUSE.** Existing Production Playbook already covers editing, sound, captions and quality gates. Test these criteria in an actual production pilot.

### 4.6 Distribution measurement closes the loop

The final module explicitly joins distribution, measurement and iteration. This aligns with the canonical FBIS→FFI feedback contract: FBIS owns metric definitions and attribution; FFI interprets supported outcomes as reusable creative-method learning.

**Disposition: REUSE.** Do not create a separate creator analytics system. Use FBIS and distinguish views/engagement from qualified demand, paid conversion and collected revenue.

## 6. Recommended controlled experiment

Use one already implemented FDG capability to produce a 45–60 second vertical explainer.

Compare two treatments of the same underlying offer and evidence:
- **A — baseline:** current FFI brief/script/visual bible/shot-list workflow.
- **B — extended:** same workflow plus (1) a written audience-psychology hypothesis, (2) a shot-to-shot continuity matrix, and (3) an explicit sound/pacing intent map.

Hold offer, CTA, channel, target audience, factual claims and approximate duration constant. Record production time, generation attempts, rejected takes, editing time, direct tool cost, continuity defects, factual defects, caption/audio QA and reviewer score. If distributed, FBIS must predefine the measurement window and attribution method. Do not infer causality from one post or treat views as revenue.

Acceptance:
- no unsupported or technically inaccurate claims;
- all required shots satisfy approved continuity invariants;
- final audio, captions, mobile legibility and CTA pass review;
- editable source assets and production receipt retained;
- actual cost and time recorded;
- outcome report separates exposure, attention, engagement, qualified leads, opportunities, paid conversions and collected revenue.

## 7. Risks, dependencies and unknowns

- Paid lessons and downloadable resources remain unreviewed.
- Tool names, underlying providers, model versions, API access, limits, export formats, data handling and licensing are not specified in sufficient public detail.
- Claims of smartphone/basic-laptop capability require task-specific testing.
- Lifetime access, updates, package entitlements, refunds and support conditions should be checked against current terms before any purchase decision.
- Testimonials and marketing figures are not independent performance evidence.
- Copyright, music, likeness/voice, client confidentiality and third-party provider terms require FLIS/FSIS review before any FDG workflow adoption.

## 8. Decision

**REUSE existing FFI architecture and Production Playbook; EXTEND the pilot with audience-psychology hypothesis, continuity matrix and sound/pacing intent map.** Do not adopt the course, its tools, its claims or its price as FDG policy. No purchase, subscription, installation or public release is authorized by this review.
