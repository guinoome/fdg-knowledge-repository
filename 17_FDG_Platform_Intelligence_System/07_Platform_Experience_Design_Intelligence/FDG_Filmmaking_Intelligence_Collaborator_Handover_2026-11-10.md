Review the public curriculum and advertised workflow of **AI Creatives Mastery** at https://aivideomastery.site/ and determine which methods materially improve FDG's existing filmmaking capability. Do not build a parallel course, prompt library or video platform.

## 2. Source boundary

The public landing page was reviewed on 2026-11-10. It advertises seven modules:
1. Storytelling, Human Psychology & Persuasion
2. Scripting & Creative Direction
3. Visual Storytelling & Shot Design
4. Visual Development & Continuity
5. AI Video Generation & Motion Direction
6. Editing, Sound Design & Pacing
7. Distribution, Measurement & Iteration

It also advertises an AI Video Editor, AI Scriptwriter, Brand Analysis, Prompt Builder, Hook/CTA generators, cheatsheets, prompt packs and monetization pathways.

**No paid lessons, member-only tools, downloadable assets or community content were accessed.** Tool behavior, technical specifications, pricing entitlements, course quality and commercial claims remain unverified. Do not imply lesson-level review or purchase. Do not bypass access controls or copy/redistribute paid course material.

## 4. Preliminary FDG assessment

The seven-stage curriculum strongly overlaps the existing Production Playbook. The source does not justify a new intelligence system or provider-specific architecture.

Recommended disposition:
- **REUSE:** existing brief, script, visual bible, storyboard, shot list, production, editing, rights, QA and FBIS measurement workflows.
- **EXTEND:** include an explicit audience-psychology hypothesis in the creative brief; a shot-to-shot continuity matrix; and a sound/pacing intent map.
- **DO NOT ADOPT YET:** the advertised AI Studio tools, prompts, course methods, production-time claims or monetization claims. Verify only through authorized access and controlled tests.
- **NO REPLACE decision:** no evidence currently shows the existing FDG playbook is inferior.

## 5. Role-specific assignments

### A. FEXIS — source and claims verification
- Recheck the canonical page and record material changes with date and source snapshot.
- Verify tool/provider identities, official documentation, model versions, API terms and source provenance if discoverable.
- Investigate whether public claims (membership, view totals, savings, production speed, client rates) have auditable evidence.
- Distinguish first-party publisher claims, independently verifiable facts and unknowns.
- Return an additive source-change record; do not claim ongoing monitoring is operational without scheduled runs, logs and recovery evidence.

### B. FFI / filmmaking specialist — craft and method
- Compare the seven-module sequence with the Production Playbook and prior source reviews.
- Determine whether audience psychology can be operationalized as a testable hypothesis rather than vague persuasion language.
- Specify a practical continuity matrix: identity, wardrobe/product configuration, environment, lighting, camera axis/movement, object state and allowed transitions.
- Define a sound/pacing intent map that ties sound, silence, transitions and rhythm to narrative purpose.
- Identify which techniques are generalizable, format-specific or unsupported.
- Propose only measurable additive edits.

### C. Creative Studio / toolchain — execution feasibility
- Map advertised tool concepts to provider-neutral interfaces: script/scene decomposition, brand profile extraction, prompt compilation, clip assembly, captioning and export.
- Do not assume the advertised AI Video Editor or other tools exist as described until tested.
- Compare editable project/source retention, clip-level control, export quality, reliability, latency, retries, cost, portability and data handling.
- Reuse current adapters and visual-craft research before proposing another provider.
- No tool installation, subscription or paid API usage without authorization.

### D. FBIS — commercial outcomes
- Define the specific FDG product/service, audience, offer and CTA for the next pilot.
- Challenge the course's monetization pathways with FDG's actual customer economics and delivery capability.
- Define attribution and baseline before distribution.
- Measure qualified leads, opportunities, quotations, paid conversions, collected revenue and cost—not views alone.
- Do not convert publisher income claims into FDG forecasts.

### E. FEIS — technical truth
- For engineering/product content, list every technical claim, diagram, number, operating state and equipment representation.
- Identify authoritative source data and reviewer.
- Establish geometry/configuration invariants and acceptance criteria.
- Label generated/simulated/illustrative visuals appropriately; generated video is not engineering evidence.
- Reject inaccurate or unsafe scenes regardless of visual quality.

### F. FLIS / FSIS — rights, privacy and security
- Review copyright, source footage, music, fonts, voice/likeness, releases, model terms and commercial use.
- Review upload handling, confidential FDG/customer data, retention, deletion and external data egress.
- Identify prohibited or restricted uses and required approvals.
- Do not upload paid course materials or confidential assets to third-party tools without authorization.

### G. FWAIS — automation and distribution
- Identify only repeatable steps after a manual workflow is validated.
- Assess API access, quotas, permissions, retries, idempotency, observability, failure recovery and rollback.
- Separate asset production from publishing. Public distribution remains separately approval-gated.
- No scraping, unauthorized account access or claims of operational automation without runtime evidence.

### H. FAIS / FMCIS / FPJIS — governance and execution
- Preserve provenance, approver, status, test record and change history.
- Convert accepted gaps into scoped work packages only after role reviews.
- Record dissent, unresolved dependencies and rejected ideas.
- Ensure the same knowledge is maintained once in its owning document and cross-linked elsewhere.

## 6. Required controlled experiment

Run a bounded A/B creative-method test on one already implemented FDG capability, using the same audience, offer, CTA, factual claims and approximate duration.

- **A — baseline:** current FFI brief → script → visual bible → storyboard/shot list → production and edit.
- **B — extension:** same baseline plus an explicit audience-psychology hypothesis, shot-to-shot continuity matrix and sound/pacing intent map.

Target output: one 45–60 second vertical video per treatment, if production capacity permits. Do not publish without explicit approval.

Record:
- time spent in briefing, scripting, generation, selection, editing and QA;
- generation attempts, rejected takes and rework;
- direct tool/API costs;
- continuity and factual defects;
- reviewer scores for clarity, narrative payoff, visual continuity, sound, captions and mobile legibility;
- if published, FBIS-defined reach, retention, engagement, clicks, qualified leads, opportunities, paid conversions and collected revenue.

Acceptance:
1. No unsupported or technically inaccurate claims.
2. Every required scene passes continuity review.
3. Sound, captions, mobile legibility and CTA pass QA.
4. Editable source assets, versions and production receipt are retained.
5. Actual time, retries and cost are recorded.
6. Any commercial conclusion is based on defined attribution and adequate evidence; a single post is not causal proof.
