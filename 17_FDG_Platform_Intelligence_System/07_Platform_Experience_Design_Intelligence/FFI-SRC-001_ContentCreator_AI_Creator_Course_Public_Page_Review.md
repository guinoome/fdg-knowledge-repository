## 4. Claims that must not be adopted without evidence

The public page makes commercial claims about speed, visual quality, reach, performance, AI ads and low-cost production. It displays student examples and creator-reported outcomes. These are publisher-provided promotional claims and testimonials, not independent evidence that FDG will achieve the same results.

- “3–4x faster”, views/follower increases, advertising performance and production-cost examples need original baselines, sample, workflow, date, paid media spend and measurement definitions.
- “Viral”, “Hollywood-level”, “better than real life”, “in minutes” and similar phrases are marketing language, not acceptance criteria.
- Tool lists and free/low-cost claims can become stale. Verify current product documentation, plan restrictions, export rights, commercial licensing, model limits and actual total cost.
- Student examples must not be treated as representative results or causal proof.

The landing page itself states outcomes are not guaranteed and individual results vary. FDG should preserve this qualification when referencing the course.

## 5. Questions for the enrolled-course review

For each lesson, capture:
1. Exact lesson/module title and sequence.
2. Exact lesson URL or timestamp/page location, where available.
3. Tool name, model/version, plan, date tested and dependencies.
4. Demonstrated input → actions → output; distinguish live demonstration from edited promotional footage.
5. Prompts, settings, reference assets and repeatability constraints.
6. What is genuinely novel versus already in FDG knowledge.
7. Failure modes: character drift, object/geometry drift, temporal inconsistency, artifacts, audio defects, hallucinated claims, watermark/export limitations.
8. Rights: input/training terms, output ownership/license, music/voice/likeness, commercial usage, privacy and retention.
9. Reproducibility: can FDG repeat it on a clean account/environment? What was cost and time per accepted result?
10. FDG disposition: adopt, adapt, test, reject, or retain as reference—with rationale and owner.
