# Purpose

This standard defines how responsibilities are assigned, governed, and maintained within the FDG Ecosystem.

Responsibilities establish accountability for engineering outcomes while preserving clear separation of organizational functions.

---

# Core Principle

Roles define purpose.

Responsibilities define accountability.

Authority enables decisions.

Execution produces outcomes.

These concepts shall remain distinct.

---

# Objectives

Responsibilities shall:

- establish accountability
- eliminate ambiguity
- prevent duplication of effort
- support delegation
- improve coordination
- strengthen organizational capability

---

# Responsibility Model

Every responsibility shall answer four questions:

1. What outcome is expected?
2. Who is accountable?
3. What authority is required?
4. How is success evaluated?

Responsibilities exist to achieve outcomes, not merely perform activities.

---

# Responsibility Categories

## Strategic Responsibilities

Define long-term direction.

Examples:

- Engineering vision
- Organizational strategy
- Capability development

---

## Governance Responsibilities

Protect organizational integrity.

Examples:

- Standard approval
- Architecture review
- Decision governance

---

## Orchestration Responsibilities

Transform objectives into executable work.

Examples:

- Work package decomposition
- Dependency management
- Collaboration coordination
- Progress monitoring

---

## Specialized Responsibilities

Provide domain expertise.

Examples:

- Mechanical engineering
- Software engineering
- Data analytics
- Financial modeling

---

## Execution Responsibilities

Deliver approved work packages.

Examples:

- Design
- Analysis
- Implementation
- Documentation
- Validation

---

## Knowledge Responsibilities

Convert validated experience into organizational intelligence.

Examples:

- Lessons learned
- Knowledge integration
- Repository updates
- Standard evolution

---

# Responsibility Rules

Every responsibility shall:

- have a clearly defined outcome
- belong to one primary role
- avoid unnecessary overlap
- support measurable evaluation where practical
- be transferable to another qualified implementation

Shared execution is acceptable.

Shared accountability should be avoided unless explicitly governed.

---

# Responsibility Assignment

Responsibilities are assigned to roles, not to individuals or technologies.

Individuals, collaborators, or systems may fulfill those roles.

Changing an implementation shall not require redesigning the organizational architecture.

---

# Responsibility Lifecycle

Responsibilities may be:

- created
- assigned
- delegated
- reviewed
- evolved
- retired

Changes shall follow controlled governance.

---

# Responsibility Quality Checklist

Before assigning a responsibility, verify:

✓ Desired outcome is defined.

✓ Accountable role identified.

✓ Required authority understood.

✓ Success criteria established.

✓ Overlap minimized.

✓ Organizational value justified.

---

# Organizational Principle

Responsibilities are organizational commitments to outcomes.

They are not descriptions of activities or tools.

---

# Continuous Improvement

Responsibilities shall evolve through operational experience, organizational learning, and controlled governance while maintaining accountability and organizational clarity.

---

# Governing Principle

Clear responsibilities create predictable engineering capability.

Ambiguous responsibilities create organizational risk.
