# Purpose

This standard defines what each canonical FDG enterprise function owns, what it does not own, its principal workflows, records, controls, interfaces, competencies, and performance responsibilities.

# 1. Executive, Strategy and Governance

Owns enterprise direction, governance architecture, major capital and structural decisions, delegated authority, portfolio priorities, policy approval, conflict resolution, and final risk acceptance where authority is reserved.

Core workflows: strategy setting; annual priorities; delegation of authority; policy approval; business-unit creation; major investment review; governance review.

Key records: strategy, objectives, authority matrix, executive decisions, enterprise policies, major approvals, risk acceptances.

Does not perform specialist engineering, accounting, recruitment, procurement, legal review, or audit merely because it approves high-impact decisions.

# 2. Engineering and Technical

Owns technical authority, engineering methods, design, calculations, specifications, technical review, asset engineering, reliability, maintenance engineering, testing and commissioning, technical acceptance, engineering standards, and technical competence requirements.

HR recruits and develops people against Engineering-defined competency requirements. Procurement purchases against Engineering specifications. Finance provides cost and investment controls. HSE establishes safety controls. Legal interprets professional and regulatory obligations. Quality assures conformance. Audit independently verifies controls.

Engineering does not own employment administration, supplier commercial negotiation, accounts payable, legal interpretation, or independent audit.

# 3. Operations and Service Delivery

Owns day-to-day delivery of approved services and operational processes, scheduling, capacity, service continuity, customer handoffs, operational SOP execution, incident coordination, service recovery, and service-level performance.

Does not own engineering design authority, commercial contracting, accounting, HR policy, or audit.

# 4. Commercial, Sales and Business Development

Owns prospect qualification, qualified opportunity pipeline, opportunity strategy, proposals, commercial offers, business development, partnerships, account development, deal progression, negotiation within delegated limits, renewals, and handoff of won work into delivery.

Marketing owns demand generation and marketing-qualified leads. Commercial owns qualified opportunities and the sales pipeline. Finance owns financial controls and revenue accounting. Legal owns contract/legal interpretation. Engineering and Service own technical/delivery feasibility.

# 5. Marketing and Growth

Owns brand governance, positioning, market communication, campaigns, content, digital presence, lead generation, acquisition experiments, market research, campaign analytics, public-facing marketing assets, nurture, and marketing-qualified lead handoff.

Does not own final sales closure, legal-claim approval, financial recognition, or technical-performance claims without domain validation.

# 6. Finance, Accounting and Treasury

Owns the financial records and financial-control system of the enterprise.

Subfunctions include financial accounting, general ledger, chart of accounts, accounts receivable, accounts payable, billing control, expense accounting, fixed assets, inventory financial valuation interfaces, project/job costing, management accounting, budgeting, forecasting, FP&A, treasury, cash management, bank reconciliation, tax accounting and filing coordination, payroll accounting/payment controls, month-end and year-end close, financial reporting, and financial-control design.

Canonical financial chain:

Chart of Accounts → Journals → Receivables → Payables → Tax → Payment Allocation → Cost Centers → Project Profitability → Financial Reporting → Cash and Management Decisions.

Finance is the accountable organizational owner of these financial meanings. FBIS is the shared business-data and intelligence architecture supporting Finance; it is not the Finance Department itself.

Core workflows include budget creation, budget check, invoicing, collection, AP three-way match, payment approval, cash forecasting, bank reconciliation, payroll funding/payment, journal posting, close, management reporting, and tax-filing coordination.

Procurement creates authorized purchasing records. Receiving proves receipt. Requesting departments confirm business or technical acceptance. HR owns employee and approved compensation inputs. Legal interprets tax/legal obligations. Audit independently tests financial controls.

Finance does not hire employees, select technical materials, provide legal opinions, or independently audit its own final controls.

# 7. Human Resources and People Operations

Owns the employee lifecycle and people-management operating system.

Subfunctions include workforce planning, headcount governance, job architecture, job descriptions, recruitment, sourcing, screening, interview coordination, offer administration, onboarding, employee master data, compensation administration, benefits, attendance/time interfaces, leave administration, employee relations, performance-management process, learning and development administration, competency records, succession planning, engagement, disciplinary-process administration, HR policy, HR records, and offboarding.

Canonical hiring chain:

Workforce Need → Approved Requisition → Job Definition → Sourcing → Screening → Department Interview/Technical Assessment → Selection → Offer/Approval → Onboarding → Probation/Performance Review → Development → Career/Retention → Separation.

HR owns the hiring process. The requesting department owns functional and technical selection criteria and participates in interviews. Finance validates budget and compensation affordability. Legal supplies employment-law requirements. Technology/Security provisions and revokes access. Approval authority approves according to delegation.

Compensation change flow: manager proposes → HR validates grade/policy/equity → Finance validates budget/payroll impact → authorized approver approves → HR updates employee/compensation master → Finance processes accounting and payment effects.

Competency development: HR owns the enterprise process, learning records and development governance; each specialist department owns its technical competency requirements and verifies technical proficiency.

Payroll boundary: HR owns employee master data, approved compensation, attendance, leave and benefits inputs. Finance owns payroll calculation, accounting and payment controls unless a later approved operating model assigns payroll processing differently. Segregation of duties shall be preserved.

HR does not own legal interpretation, engineering authority, departmental work assignment, accounting, or independent audit.

# 8. Procurement, Purchasing and Supply Chain

Owns enterprise sourcing and purchase execution from approved requirement through supplier performance while preserving the originating department's technical or operational ownership.

Subfunctions include purchase-requisition control, sourcing, RFQ/tender, bid comparison, supplier qualification, approved vendor list, commercial negotiation, purchase orders, contract-routing coordination, expediting, supplier performance, supply risk, category/strategic sourcing, logistics coordination, receiving interface, inventory replenishment interface, and procurement records.

Canonical cross-functional chain:

Need Owner → PR/SRF → Approval/Budget Check → Procurement Sourcing → Supplier Qualification → RFQ/Tender → Technical/Commercial Evaluation → PO/Contract → Expediting → Delivery → Receiving → Technical/User Acceptance → Supplier Invoice → Finance AP Match → Payment → Supplier Performance Review.

Supplier qualification is led by Procurement. Technical departments validate technical capability and suitability. Finance may evaluate financial/payment risk. Legal reviews contractual/legal requirements. HSE evaluates contractor safety where applicable. Quality may validate quality-system requirements.

Procurement owns the approved vendor list and supplier commercial record. It does not own the technical specification, accounting ledger, or legal interpretation.

# 9. Project and Portfolio Management

Owns project governance, initiation, planning, scope integration, schedule, cross-functional coordination, project risk and issue control, budget coordination, progress reporting, change control, stage gates, closeout, lessons learned, and portfolio visibility.

Technical authority remains with Engineering. Accounting remains with Finance. Contracting remains with Legal, Commercial, or Procurement according to subject.

# 10. Quality and Continuous Improvement

Owns the quality-management system, process quality, QA/QC frameworks, nonconformance governance, CAPA coordination, verification/validation methods, quality records, process capability, and continuous-improvement methods.

Quality may verify work but does not replace the producing function's accountability. Internal Audit remains independent.

# 11. Enterprise Risk Management

Owns the enterprise risk framework, taxonomy, consolidated risk register, assessment methods, risk reporting, appetite/threshold administration, control-mapping coordination, and risk escalation.

Risk owners remain in the departments that create or manage the underlying risk. Internal Audit independently assesses risk-management effectiveness.

# 12. Internal Audit and Assurance

Owns independent assurance over governance, risk, controls, financial/commercial processes, operations, engineering, projects, legal/compliance interfaces, security, software, agents, workflows, quality, evidence, and remediation verification.

FAIS is the canonical intelligence system supporting this function.

Internal Audit does not own the processes it audits, approve routine operational transactions it later audits, or silently remediate findings.

# 13. Legal and Compliance

Owns legal intelligence, legal interpretation and escalation, contracts and clauses, corporate/business compliance, regulatory obligations, employment-law requirements, procurement/commercial law, IP, privacy law, professional liability, disputes/claims, legal risk, and regulatory-change intelligence.

FLIS is the canonical intelligence system supporting this function.

Legal does not recruit employees, operate procurement, run payroll, perform accounting, manage engineering execution, or own security controls. It supplies legal and regulatory requirements those departments implement.

# 14. Technology, Data and Cybersecurity

Owns enterprise technology operations, platform engineering governance, data architecture, integration, identity/access implementation, cybersecurity operations, device/system administration, backups, resilience, disaster recovery, software/service lifecycle, technical support, and technology-vendor coordination.

FSIS governs security intelligence and controls; FPIS governs platform intelligence; FWAIS supports automation. Business and engineering departments remain owners of their business or technical process meaning.

# 15. Corporate Services and Administration

Owns general administrative services that support the enterprise but are not specialist HR, Finance, Legal, Procurement, Technology, Engineering or Operations responsibilities.

Subfunctions may include office administration, records coordination, meeting/logistics support, travel administration, general correspondence, corporate calendars, common supplies coordination, facility-administration interface, permits/renewal coordination support, reception, common asset custody, and executive administrative support.

Corporate Services does not become a catch-all owner for specialist responsibilities.

# 16. Health, Safety, Environment and Sustainability

Owns occupational health and safety governance, workplace hazard management, incident reporting/investigation coordination, emergency preparedness, contractor safety requirements, environmental-compliance coordination, sustainability controls, safety-training governance, and HSE performance reporting.

Engineering and Operations remain accountable for safe technical execution. HR supports people records and training administration. Legal interprets applicable obligations. Procurement enforces contractor/supplier requirements. Audit independently verifies control effectiveness.

# Governing Rule

When ownership is unclear, assign the responsibility to the function whose professional specialty determines the correctness of the outcome, then define the interfaces required from other functions.
