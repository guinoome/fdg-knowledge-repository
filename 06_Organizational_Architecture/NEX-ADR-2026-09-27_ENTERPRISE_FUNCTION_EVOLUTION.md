# Previous Baseline

NEX-STD-036 defined a strong engineering-centered organizational architecture using capability, roles, authority, delegation, collaboration, interfaces, work packages and continuous improvement.

Subsequent intelligence systems accumulated substantial finance, commercial, employee, procurement, legal, security, service, project, quality, risk, audit and operational knowledge, but the repository did not explicitly reconcile those systems into a canonical enterprise department/function model.

# Problem Identified

The repository could answer many domain questions but could not consistently answer who organizationally owns accounts payable, hiring, salary changes, supplier qualification, approved vendors, purchase orders, financial close, cash forecasting, marketing pipeline, employee competency development, or independent audit.

Finance primitives in FBIS could be interpreted as only future software architecture rather than organizational knowledge of the Finance Department.

Employment-law knowledge in FLIS and workforce knowledge in FBPOIS/FMIS could be misread as substitutes for Human Resources even though HR owns hiring, benefits, employee lifecycle and people operations.

Procurement knowledge was distributed across FBIS, FLIS and FMIS without an explicit enterprise Procurement owner.

# Founder Clarification

The Founder directed that enterprise functions remain separate specialties that reconcile through defined interfaces.

Example: Engineering raises a need and defines the technical requirement; Procurement executes sourcing and purchasing; Finance executes payable and payment controls; Engineering receives the result for technical acceptance.

The Founder also directed that prior knowledge must not be deleted. The repository must preserve traceability showing why the architecture evolved.

# Decision

1. Establish a canonical FDG Enterprise Operating Model under 06_Organizational_Architecture.
2. Recognize 16 enterprise functions with explicit ownership and boundaries.
3. Treat intelligence systems as supporting knowledge/intelligence architectures, not substitutes for organizational departments.
4. Recognize Finance, HR, Procurement, Audit, Legal, Engineering and the other enterprise functions as distinct organizational specialties.
5. Separate organizational knowledge maturity from operational deployment and staffing state.
6. Classify the enterprise-function architecture as Very Strong in Organizational Knowledge Maturity while requiring separate evidence for real-world execution maturity.
7. Preserve previous baselines and append cross-references rather than deleting historical knowledge.
