# Purpose

This standard establishes the canonical enterprise-function layer of the FDG Ecosystem. It converts distributed business, engineering, legal, audit, service, security, project, platform, workforce, procurement, finance, marketing, and administrative knowledge into an explicit enterprise operating model without deleting prior knowledge.

# Core Principle

An intelligence system is not a department. A department is an accountable organizational function. Intelligence systems preserve, support, automate, analyze, govern, or audit the knowledge used by those functions.

A department may exist as a fully defined organizational capability before it has dedicated headcount. Execution may be founder-held, assigned to an employee, delegated to a collaborator, outsourced to a professional provider, automation-assisted, or split across authorized implementers.

Department existence, knowledge maturity, operational deployment, and staffing state shall be tracked separately.

These are enterprise functions, not mandatory present-day headcount boxes. They may later become departments, teams, shared services, subsidiaries, outsourced functions, or federated operating units as scale requires.

# Separation of Specialties

Each function owns its specialty. Connected workflows do not transfer ownership of one specialty into another.

Example:

Engineering identifies and technically specifies a spare part.
Procurement owns sourcing, supplier competition, commercial comparison, purchase-order execution, and supplier follow-up.
Legal supplies contract and commercial-law requirements when needed.
Finance validates budget, payable, tax, cash, and payment controls.
Warehouse or Receiving records physical receipt and custody.
Engineering performs technical acceptance.
Internal Audit may independently test whether the controls worked.

No participating function becomes the owner of all other steps.

# Enterprise Workflow Rule

Every material cross-functional workflow should distinguish:

- originating need owner
- specialist process owner
- approval authority
- control owner
- record owner
- execution role
- acceptance role
- independent assurance role where applicable

# Department Knowledge Rule

The knowledge of a department includes its mandate, responsibilities, authority, workflows, policies, records, controls, interfaces, competencies, KPIs, risks, decision rights, escalation paths, and improvement mechanisms.

Accordingly, Finance knowledge is organizational knowledge of the Finance, Accounting and Treasury function. HR knowledge is organizational knowledge of Human Resources and People Operations. Procurement knowledge is organizational knowledge of Procurement, Purchasing and Supply Chain.

Supporting systems such as FBIS, FLIS, FAIS, FSIS, FSvIS, FEIS, FBPOIS, FPJIS, FPIS and FWAIS support these functions without erasing their organizational boundaries.

# Staffing States

A function may be Founder-held, Shared-role, Dedicated internal, Outsourced, Hybrid, Automation-assisted, or Temporarily unstaffed without changing its canonical responsibility boundary.

# Governance

New enterprise functions require Founder approval and must demonstrate a responsibility that cannot be cleanly owned by an existing function. Subfunctions should be added inside existing functions before creating new top-level intelligence systems or organizational domains.

# Knowledge Preservation

NEX-STD-036 remains preserved as the earlier organizational baseline. This standard extends it from an engineering-centered organizational model into an enterprise-wide operating model.
