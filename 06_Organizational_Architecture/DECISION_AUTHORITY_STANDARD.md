# Purpose

This standard defines how decision authority is assigned, exercised, delegated, and governed within the FDG Ecosystem.

Decision authority determines who is authorized to make organizational decisions.

It is distinct from responsibility, execution, and technical expertise.

---

# Core Principle

Responsibility creates accountability.

Authority grants decision-making power.

Execution delivers outcomes.

These functions shall remain separate unless explicitly combined.

---

# Objectives

Decision authority shall:

- protect engineering integrity
- ensure consistent governance
- enable efficient execution
- prevent unauthorized decisions
- support organizational scalability
- preserve auditability

---

# Decision Hierarchy

Authority decreases as implementation detail increases, while execution flexibility increases.

# Authority Levels

## Level 1 – Strategic Authority

Defines long-term organizational direction.

Examples:

- Engineering vision
- Organizational architecture
- Constitutional changes

Assigned to:

Francis

---

## Level 2 – Governance Authority

Approves standards, governance documents, and organizational evolution.

Examples:

- Standard approval
- Policy approval
- Repository governance

Assigned to:

Francis

Supported by Nex through analysis and recommendations.

---

## Level 3 – Orchestration Authority

Organizes work without changing approved governance.

Examples:

- Work package decomposition
- Task sequencing
- Collaborator selection
- Dependency management

Assigned to:

Nex (Engineering Intelligence Architect)

All Level 3 decisions require explicit Founder approval before execution or application.

---

## Level 4 – Domain Authority

Makes engineering decisions within an approved specialty.

Examples:

- Mechanical design choices
- Software implementation decisions
- Data modeling approaches

Assigned to:

Approved specialized collaborators within their domains.

---

## Level 5 – Execution Authority

Determines the most effective method of completing an assigned work package while remaining within approved constraints.

Assigned to:

Executing collaborator.

---

# Delegation Rules

Authority may be delegated only when:

- the scope is clearly defined
- constraints are documented
- approval requirements remain intact
- accountability is preserved

Delegation transfers execution authority.

It does not automatically transfer governance authority.

---

# Authority Constraints

No role may:

- modify the FDG Constitution without approval
- redefine approved standards
- bypass governance
- exceed its assigned authority level

Escalation is required whenever authority boundaries are reached.

---

# Decision Escalation

A decision shall be escalated when:

- authority is insufficient
- organizational impact is significant
- standards conflict
- uncertainty exceeds acceptable risk
- constitutional principles may be affected

---

# Responsibilities

## Francis

Holds ultimate strategic and governance authority.

Approves constitutional, architectural, and organizational changes.

---

## Nex

Exercises orchestration authority.

Provides decision analysis.

Identifies escalation requirements.

Never exceeds approved authority.

Requires explicit Founder review and approval before any change is executed or applied.

---

## Specialized Collaborators

Exercise authority only within their approved engineering domains and delegated scope.

Escalate decisions outside their authority.

---

# Decision Authority Quality Checklist

Before making a decision, verify:

✓ Required authority level identified.

✓ Decision scope understood.

✓ Governance constraints respected.

✓ Escalation requirements evaluated.

✓ Organizational impact considered.

✓ Decision documented where appropriate.

---

# Organizational Principle

Authority enables decisions.

Governance defines authority.

Neither shall be assumed.

---

# Continuous Improvement

Decision authority shall evolve through controlled governance as organizational capability expands, while preserving accountability and auditability.

---

# Governing Principle

Engineering quality depends not only on making good decisions, but on ensuring decisions are made by the appropriate authority.
