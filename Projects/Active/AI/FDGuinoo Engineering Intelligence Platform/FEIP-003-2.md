# 13. Discipline Interdependencies

Engineering disciplines rarely operate independently.

The platform shall recognize technical dependencies while preserving ownership boundaries.

## 13.1 Mechanical Engineering

Primary dependencies:

- Structural Engineering
- Electrical Engineering
- Plumbing Engineering
- Fire Protection Engineering
- Facility Engineering

Typical shared information:

- Equipment loads
- Equipment locations
- Pipe routing
- Utility requirements
- Structural supports
- Electrical demand

---

## 13.2 Electrical Engineering

Primary dependencies:

- Mechanical Engineering
- Solar Engineering
- Facility Engineering
- Fire Protection Engineering

Typical shared information:

- Motor schedules
- Equipment power demand
- Generator sizing
- Distribution coordination
- Control systems

---

## 13.3 Plumbing Engineering

Primary dependencies:

- Mechanical Engineering
- Civil Engineering
- Fire Protection Engineering

Typical shared information:

- Water demand
- Storage tanks
- Drainage
- Utility routing
- Pump requirements

---

## 13.4 Fire Protection Engineering

Primary dependencies:

- Mechanical Engineering
- Plumbing Engineering
- Civil Engineering
- Structural Engineering

Typical shared information:

- Fire water storage
- Pump rooms
- Pipe routing
- Structural penetrations
- Utility coordination

## 13.5 Facility Engineering

Facility Engineering functions as an operational discipline integrating information from every engineering domain after commissioning.

Typical responsibilities include:

- Preventive Maintenance
- Corrective Maintenance
- Asset Management
- Condition Assessment
- Operational Analytics
- Energy Monitoring
- Lifecycle Planning

# 14. Engineering Module Ownership

Each engineering module shall have a clearly defined owner responsible for technical governance.

Ownership includes:

- engineering formulas;
- engineering standards;
- validation rules;
- report templates;
- workflow definitions;
- acceptance testing.

Ownership does not imply exclusive data ownership.

Shared project information remains centrally managed.

# 15. Calculation Library Organization

Calculation libraries shall be organized hierarchically.

Example:

```
Mechanical Engineering
│
├── Fluid Mechanics
├── Thermodynamics
├── Heat Transfer
├── HVAC
├── Refrigeration
├── Pumps
├── Fans
├── Boilers
├── Steam Systems
└── Compressed Air

Electrical Engineering
│
├── Load Analysis
├── Cable Sizing
├── Transformer Design
├── Generator Design
├── Voltage Drop
├── Fault Current
└── Lighting

Fire Protection Engineering
│
├── Fire Pumps
├── Sprinklers
├── Standpipes
├── Water Storage
└── Hydraulic Analysis
```

Each calculation shall maintain:

- unique identifier;
- version number;
- engineering discipline;
- supported standards;
- validation history;
- revision history.

---

# 16. Standards Library Organization

Engineering standards shall be centrally managed rather than duplicated across modules.

Each standards record shall contain:

- Standard Identifier
- Title
- Governing Organization
- Edition
- Publication Date
- Effective Date
- Supported Modules
- Supported Calculations
- Validation Status
- Review Date
- Deprecation Status

Calculations shall reference standards by identifier instead of embedding standard-specific values directly within calculation logic.

---

# 17. Equipment Library Structure

The Equipment Library shall provide a shared repository of engineering equipment used throughout FEIP.

Minimum classification hierarchy:

```
Equipment Category
        │
Equipment Family
        │
Equipment Type
        │
Manufacturer
        │
Model
        │
Technical Specifications
        │
Performance Curves
        │
Engineering Documentation
```

Each equipment record shall support:

- technical specifications;
- performance characteristics;
- dimensional information;
- operating limits;
- maintenance information;
- related calculations.

Equipment definitions shall remain independent of individual projects.

---

# 18. Engineering Report Framework

Every engineering discipline shall utilize a common reporting framework while supporting discipline-specific content.

A standard engineering report shall include:

- Cover Page
- Revision History
- Project Information
- Design Basis
- Assumptions
- Engineering Inputs
- Engineering Calculations
- Results
- Compliance Summary
- Engineering Conclusions
- References
- Appendices

This structure promotes consistency across all engineering disciplines.
