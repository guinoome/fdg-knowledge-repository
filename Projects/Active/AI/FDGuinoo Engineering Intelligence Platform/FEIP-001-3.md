# Product Philosophy & Engineering Design Principles
## Part 3 of N

---

# 18. Security Philosophy

Security within FEIP shall protect engineering information, business data, intellectual property, and operational continuity without unnecessarily restricting legitimate engineering work.

Security is considered an engineering quality attribute rather than an independent feature.

---

## 18.1 Security Objectives

The security architecture shall ensure:

- Confidentiality of engineering and business information.
- Integrity of engineering calculations and records.
- Availability of critical platform services.
- Accountability of all user activities.
- Non-repudiation of engineering approvals.
- Protection of proprietary engineering knowledge.

---

## 18.2 Security by Design

Security requirements shall be incorporated during design rather than introduced after implementation.

Every new module shall include:

- authentication requirements;
- authorization model;
- audit requirements;
- input validation;
- data protection strategy;
- API security;
- error handling;
- logging strategy.

A module shall not be considered complete without satisfying its defined security requirements.

---

## 18.3 Least Privilege Principle

Users shall receive only the permissions required to perform their assigned responsibilities.

Permissions shall be based on:

- organizational role;
- engineering discipline;
- project assignment;
- module subscription;
- administrative delegation.

Temporary privilege elevation shall be time-limited, logged, and auditable.

---

## 18.4 Defense in Depth

The platform shall employ multiple independent layers of protection.

Typical layers include:

- Identity Management
- Multi-Factor Authentication
- Role-Based Access Control
- API Authorization
- Encryption
- Network Security
- Audit Logging
- Backup and Recovery
- Monitoring and Alerting

Failure of one control shall not expose the entire platform.

---

# 19. Compliance Philosophy

Compliance is a continuous operational responsibility.

The objective is to assist engineers in maintaining alignment with applicable engineering standards while recognizing that final professional responsibility remains with the engineer of record.

---

## 19.1 Standards Awareness

Engineering calculations shall identify, where applicable:

- governing engineering standard;
- supported edition;
- publication year;
- FEIP validation version;
- review date.

This information shall be visible within the calculation interface and exported reports where relevant.

---

## 19.2 Standards Versioning

Engineering standards evolve over time.

FEIP shall maintain versioned support for standards so that historical projects remain traceable to the edition under which they were executed.

Example:

Project A

↓

ASHRAE Edition X

Project B

↓

ASHRAE Edition Y

The platform shall preserve the historical engineering basis for completed work.

---

## 19.3 Compliance Assistance

FEIP may provide:

- warnings;
- validation messages;
- recommended corrections;
- missing requirement notifications;
- engineering checklists.

However, the platform shall avoid automatically certifying regulatory compliance unless explicitly authorized by documented rules.

---

# 20. Quality Management Philosophy

Quality within FEIP extends beyond software reliability.

The platform shall promote quality in engineering outputs, engineering processes, and organizational operations.

---

## 20.1 Quality Objectives

FEIP shall support:

- repeatable engineering workflows;
- standardized documentation;
- validated calculations;
- controlled revisions;
- engineering review processes;
- measurable project performance.

---

## 20.2 Prevention over Correction

The platform shall emphasize early detection of issues rather than post-completion correction.

Examples include:

- incomplete engineering inputs;
- inconsistent equipment selection;
- conflicting project information;
- missing engineering documents;
- unrealistic operating parameters.

Preventing engineering errors is preferred over correcting them after project execution.

---

## 20.3 Continuous Improvement

Engineering quality shall improve through:

- user feedback;
- standards updates;
- engineering reviews;
- performance analytics;
- defect analysis;
- operational metrics.

The platform architecture shall support iterative enhancement without disrupting existing projects.

---

# 21. Standards Governance

Engineering standards are foundational knowledge assets within FEIP.

---

## 21.1 Supported Standards

The platform architecture shall support engineering standards from multiple organizations, including those adopted by user organizations or regulatory authorities.

Each supported standard shall maintain associated metadata, including:

- title;
- edition;
- publication date;
- governing organization;
- applicable disciplines;
- implementation status;
- validation history.

---

## 21.2 Quarterly Review Framework

At least once every calendar quarter, the engineering content shall undergo structured review.

The review process shall include:

- newly issued standards;
- revised standards;
- withdrawn standards;
- engineering interpretation updates;
- calculation verification;
- documentation updates.

The outcome of each review shall be recorded within the platform release history.

---

## 21.3 Deprecation Policy

When a supported engineering standard becomes obsolete:

- historical projects shall remain accessible;
- historical calculations shall remain reproducible;
- new projects shall default to the latest supported edition unless configured otherwise.

No historical engineering record shall be invalidated solely due to publication of a newer standard.

---

# 22. Documentation Philosophy

Documentation is a core engineering deliverable.

Software documentation shall be treated with the same importance as engineering calculations and project records.

---

## 22.1 Documentation Objectives

Documentation shall provide:

- engineering traceability;
- implementation guidance;
- operational consistency;
- maintainability;
- knowledge preservation.

---

## 22.2 Documentation Hierarchy

Documentation shall follow a structured hierarchy.

Product Charter

↓

Business Specifications

↓

Software Requirements

↓

Architecture Specifications

↓

Database Specifications

↓

API Specifications

↓

UI Specifications

↓

Development Standards

↓

Testing Specifications

↓

Operations Documentation

Every lower-level document shall trace back to higher-level requirements.

---

## 22.3 Documentation Completeness

Specifications shall define:

- business intent;
- functional behavior;
- validation rules;
- permissions;
- interfaces;
- dependencies;
- acceptance criteria.

Implementation shall not rely on undocumented assumptions.

---

# 23. Configuration Management Philosophy

Configuration shall replace hard-coded behavior wherever practical.

Examples of configurable elements include:

- engineering units;
- currency;
- tax rules;
- organizational branding;
- workflow approvals;
- engineering tolerances;
- notification preferences;
- report templates.

Configuration changes shall not require source code modification for routine operational adjustments.

---

## 23.1 Configuration Hierarchy

Configuration precedence shall follow:

Platform Defaults

↓

Organization Settings

↓

Project Settings

↓

User Preferences

Higher-level settings may restrict lower-level customization where necessary to preserve engineering consistency.

---

# 24. Change Management Philosophy

Engineering software evolves continuously.

All changes shall follow controlled processes.

---

## 24.1 Types of Change

Changes include:

- feature enhancements;
- engineering standard updates;
- calculation improvements;
- security updates;
- database modifications;
- API revisions;
- workflow improvements;
- user interface refinements.

Each change shall be documented and traceable.

---

## 24.2 Backward Compatibility

Where practical, new versions shall preserve compatibility with existing projects.

Breaking changes require:

- documented justification;
- migration guidance;
- impact assessment;
- approval before implementation.

---

## 24.3 Revision Control

Every engineering specification shall maintain:

- version identifier;
- revision history;
- approval status;
- effective date;
- superseded references.

Historical revisions shall remain available for audit purposes.

---

# 25. Engineering Calculation Principles

Engineering calculations represent one of the highest-risk components of FEIP.

Accordingly, every calculation engine shall comply with common governing principles.

---

## 25.1 Verification

Engineering calculations shall be:

- independently verified during development;
- tested using representative engineering cases;
- regression tested after modifications;
- traceable to documented methodologies.

---

## 25.2 Reproducibility

Given identical inputs, configuration, and standards edition, the platform shall produce identical engineering outputs.

Calculation behavior shall be deterministic.

---

## 25.3 Explainability

Users shall be able to review:

- governing equations;
- applied coefficients;
- selected assumptions;
- engineering references;
- intermediate values where appropriate.

Engineering calculations shall not function as unexplained "black boxes."

---

# 26. Audit Philosophy

Every significant engineering and business action shall be auditable.

Audit records shall support:

- engineering investigations;
- operational reviews;
- security analysis;
- compliance verification;
- historical reconstruction.

Audit logs shall be tamper-evident and retained according to organizational policy.
