# 10. Calculation Engine

## 10.1 Purpose

The Calculation Engine is the authoritative computational engine of FEIP.

Its responsibility is to perform deterministic engineering calculations using validated engineering methods while remaining independent of optimization, compliance evaluation, business workflows, and engineering recommendations.

Every engineering calculation within FEIP shall originate from this engine.

---

## 10.2 Objectives

The Calculation Engine shall:

- Produce repeatable engineering results.
- Support multidisciplinary engineering calculations.
- Centralize engineering formulas.
- Eliminate duplicate implementations.
- Maintain version-controlled calculation methods.
- Support report generation.
- Support API consumption.
- Preserve engineering traceability.

---

## 10.3 Responsibilities

The Calculation Engine owns:

- Engineering equations
- Engineering formulas
- Numerical computation
- Unit conversion
- Physical property lookup
- Engineering constants
- Intermediate calculations
- Result generation
- Calculation metadata
- Calculation history

It shall not own:

- Optimization
- Cost analysis
- Compliance interpretation
- Engineering recommendations
- Workflow management
- Project management

These responsibilities belong to other CORE engines.

---

# 12. Calculation Metadata

Every calculation record shall include metadata for traceability.

Minimum metadata includes:

| Field | Description |
|--------|-------------|
| Calculation ID | Unique identifier |
| Module | Originating module |
| Engineering Discipline | Discipline classification |
| Calculation Type | Type of engineering calculation |
| Formula Version | Version identifier |
| Standards Version | Applicable engineering standard |
| Created By | User |
| Organization | Tenant |
| Project | Associated project |
| Timestamp | Creation date |
| Revision | Revision number |
| Status | Draft, Reviewed, Approved, Archived |

No calculation shall exist without metadata.

# 13. Optimization Engine

## 13.1 Purpose

The Optimization Engine evaluates engineering solutions to identify improved alternatives based on configurable objectives.

It does not alter engineering formulas or replace engineering judgment.

Instead, it analyzes valid engineering solutions generated by the Calculation Engine.

---

## 13.2 Objectives

The engine shall support optimization of:

- Energy consumption
- Equipment efficiency
- Operating cost
- Capital cost
- Lifecycle cost
- Water consumption
- Carbon emissions
- Equipment utilization
- System redundancy
- Space utilization

---

## 13.3 Responsibilities

The Optimization Engine owns:

- Comparative analysis
- Scenario evaluation
- Value engineering
- Operational optimization
- Equipment comparison
- Sensitivity analysis
- Engineering trade-off analysis

It shall not own:

- Engineering formulas
- Compliance checking
- Engineering approvals
- Engineering reports

---

## 13.4 Optimization Workflow

```
Calculation Results

↓

Optimization Criteria

↓

Constraint Evaluation

↓

Scenario Generation

↓

Engineering Comparison

↓

Ranking

↓

Optimization Summary

↓

Engineering Recommendation
```

Multiple optimization objectives may be evaluated simultaneously.

---

## 13.5 Example

Pump Selection

Scenario A

- Lower capital cost
- Higher operating cost

Scenario B

- Higher capital cost
- Lower operating cost

Scenario C

- Variable speed configuration

The engine compares alternatives using configured engineering and financial criteria, then provides ranked options for engineer review.

The final selection remains the responsibility of the engineer.

---

# 14. Optimization Strategies

Supported strategies include:

- Minimum lifecycle cost
- Minimum operating cost
- Maximum efficiency
- Minimum energy consumption
- Minimum water consumption
- Maximum reliability
- Minimum maintenance effort
- Maximum redundancy
- Minimum carbon footprint
- User-defined objective functions

Strategies shall be configurable by organization where appropriate.

---

# 15. Optimization Constraints

Optimization shall respect engineering constraints, including:

- Regulatory requirements
- Engineering standards
- Equipment limitations
- Design safety factors
- User-defined limits
- Budget constraints
- Space constraints
- Environmental conditions

Optimization shall never recommend solutions that violate mandatory engineering requirements.
