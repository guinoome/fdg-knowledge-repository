# 12. Engineering Decision Principles

Every engineering decision implemented within FEIP shall follow a structured hierarchy to ensure technical consistency across all disciplines and modules.

The objective is to eliminate arbitrary implementation decisions and maintain a defensible engineering basis for every calculation, workflow, report, recommendation, and business process.

---

## 12.1 Decision Hierarchy

Engineering decisions shall follow the order below.

| Priority | Decision Authority |
|----------|--------------------|
| 1 | Public Safety |
| 2 | Applicable Laws and Regulations |
| 3 | Engineering Standards |
| 4 | Approved Engineering Practices |
| 5 | FEIP Engineering Specifications |
| 6 | Organizational Standards |
| 7 | Project Requirements |
| 8 | User Configuration |
| 9 | Personal Preferences |

If conflicts exist between two levels, the higher authority shall prevail unless an approved engineering deviation exists.

---

## 12.2 Engineering Judgment

FEIP shall support engineering judgment rather than replace it.

Examples include:

- recommending pipe sizes rather than forcing selections;
- suggesting pump configurations while allowing engineer review;
- highlighting potential code violations without automatically approving designs;
- identifying design inconsistencies for engineering evaluation.

Final engineering responsibility remains with the licensed engineer or authorized organization.

---

## 12.3 Assumptions

Engineering assumptions significantly influence design outcomes.

Whenever FEIP applies assumptions, the platform shall:

- identify each assumption explicitly;
- indicate whether it is user-defined or system-defined;
- display the engineering basis where applicable;
- include assumptions within exported reports.

Hidden assumptions are prohibited.

---

## 12.4 Transparency

Every engineering output shall be explainable.

Where practical, FEIP shall display:

- governing equations;
- referenced standards;
- design criteria;
- calculation methodology;
- intermediate results;
- applied safety factors;
- limiting conditions.

Users shall understand how results were obtained without reverse engineering the software.

---

# 13. Business Design Principles

FEIP is also a business operating system.

Business functionality shall always complement engineering workflows rather than compete with them.

---

## 13.1 Engineering-Centric Business Operations

Business modules shall originate from engineering information whenever possible.

Examples:

Project Creation

↓

Engineering Design

↓

Material Quantities

↓

Cost Estimate

↓

Budget

↓

Procurement

↓

Construction

↓

Commissioning

↓

Asset Registration

↓

Preventive Maintenance

↓

Executive Reporting

Engineering information shall initiate downstream business processes instead of requiring duplicate manual data entry.

---

## 13.2 Elimination of Duplicate Work

The platform shall minimize repetitive tasks.

Examples include:

- equipment entered once;
- project information entered once;
- client information entered once;
- calculations automatically linked to reports;
- estimates linked to procurement;
- maintenance linked to installed assets.

Duplicate data entry is considered a design defect unless technically unavoidable.

---

## 13.3 Engineering Traceability

Every engineering artifact shall remain connected throughout its lifecycle.

Example:

↓

Project

↓

Calculations

↓

Drawings

↓

Specifications

↓

Procurement

↓

Installation

↓

Commissioning

↓

Maintenance

↓

Replacement

↓

Historical Archive

This traceability enables complete engineering history throughout the asset lifecycle.

# 14. User Experience Philosophy

User interface decisions shall prioritize engineering efficiency over visual novelty.

The objective is to reduce cognitive load while supporting technically complex work.

---

## 14.1 Professional Workspace

The interface shall resemble professional engineering software rather than consumer applications.

Characteristics include:

- information-dense layouts;
- configurable workspaces;
- persistent navigation;
- keyboard accessibility;
- engineering terminology;
- consistent workflows.

---

## 14.2 Reduced Navigation

Frequently used engineering functions shall require minimal navigation.

Engineering calculations should generally be accessible within:

- three clicks, or
- a global search command.

---

## 14.3 Context Preservation

Users shall not lose work due to navigation.

Whenever practical, FEIP shall preserve:

- unsaved forms;
- calculation inputs;
- report drafts;
- filter selections;
- workspace layouts.

---

## 14.4 Progressive Complexity

Interfaces shall expose information according to user needs.

For example:

Student

- simplified engineering interface.

Professional Engineer

- full engineering interface.

Enterprise Administrator

- operational dashboards.

Engineering Specialist

- advanced calculation controls.

This allows the platform to remain approachable while supporting expert workflows.

# 15. Data Governance Principles

Engineering data is a strategic organizational asset.

The platform shall manage data with the same rigor applied to engineering documentation.

---

## 15.1 Data Ownership

Every record shall have a clearly defined owner.

Examples include:

| Record | Owner |
|---------|------|
| Client | Client Module |
| Equipment | Equipment Library |
| Project | Project Module |
| Calculation | Calculation Engine |
| Report | Report Repository |
| Asset | Asset Management |
| User | Identity Management |

Ownership determines validation rules, permissions, and lifecycle management.

---

## 15.2 Data Integrity

FEIP shall preserve data integrity through:

- validation rules;
- referential integrity;
- controlled updates;
- audit logging;
- version history;
- transaction consistency.

Direct modification outside approved interfaces is prohibited.

---

## 15.3 Data Lifecycle

Engineering information shall transition through defined lifecycle states.

Typical lifecycle:

Draft

↓

Under Review

↓

Approved

↓

Issued

↓

Superseded

↓

Archived

Each transition shall follow documented business rules.

---

## 15.4 Immutable Engineering Records

Certain engineering records become immutable after approval.

Examples include:

- approved calculation reports;
- signed engineering documents;
- issued revisions;
- official project deliverables;
- compliance certificates.

Subsequent changes require a new revision rather than modification of historical records.

---

# 16. Software Architecture Principles

The software architecture shall support long-term scalability and maintainability.

---

## 16.1 Modular Architecture

Each module shall:

- own a clearly defined responsibility;
- expose documented interfaces;
- minimize dependencies;
- support independent maintenance;
- permit future replacement without platform redesign.

---

## 16.2 Separation of Concerns

Business logic, presentation logic, persistence, and engineering calculations shall remain independent.

Typical separation:

Presentation Layer

↓

Application Services

↓

Business Logic

↓

CORE Engineering Services

↓

Data Layer

↓

Infrastructure

No user interface shall directly execute engineering calculations.

---

## 16.3 Loose Coupling

Modules shall communicate through stable interfaces.

Examples:

Project Module

↓

Calculation Service

↓

Equipment Service

↓

Reporting Service

↓

Notification Service

Direct database dependency between unrelated modules is discouraged.

---

## 16.4 Extensibility

Future engineering disciplines shall be incorporated without redesigning the platform architecture.

Examples include:

- mining engineering;
- aerospace engineering;
- railway engineering;
- environmental engineering;
- biomedical engineering.

The architecture shall anticipate expansion beyond Version 1.

---
