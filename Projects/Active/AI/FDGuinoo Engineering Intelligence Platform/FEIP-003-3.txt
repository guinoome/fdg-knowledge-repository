# 19. Discipline Governance

Each engineering discipline shall operate under a formal governance model to ensure technical consistency, quality, and accountability throughout the FEIP platform.

Governance applies to:

- Engineering calculations
- Standards implementation
- Equipment libraries
- Material libraries
- Report templates
- Workflow definitions
- Validation rules
- Engineering terminology
- Discipline-specific user interfaces

---

## 19.1 Discipline Steward

Each discipline shall have an assigned Discipline Steward.

The Discipline Steward is responsible for:

- approving engineering methodologies;
- validating calculation implementations;
- reviewing standards updates;
- maintaining engineering terminology;
- approving discipline-specific templates;
- coordinating cross-discipline technical issues.

A Discipline Steward may be supported by multiple Subject Matter Experts (SMEs).

---

## 19.2 Subject Matter Experts (SMEs)

SMEs provide technical expertise within specialized domains.

Examples:

Mechanical Engineering

- HVAC
- Refrigeration
- Pumps
- Steam Systems
- Compressed Air

Electrical Engineering

- Power Systems
- Lighting
- Protection Systems
- Renewable Energy

Fire Protection Engineering

- Fire Hydraulics
- Fire Pumps
- Detection Systems
- Suppression Systems

SMEs review engineering content but do not independently modify platform architecture.

---

# 20. Engineering Knowledge Ownership

Engineering knowledge shall be treated as a governed asset.

Every knowledge object shall have:

- an owner;
- a version;
- a validation status;
- an approval history;
- a review schedule.

Knowledge objects include:

- engineering formulas;
- engineering constants;
- material properties;
- equipment specifications;
- engineering guidance;
- report templates;
- standards interpretations.

---

## 20.1 Knowledge Lifecycle

Every engineering knowledge object shall progress through a controlled lifecycle.

```
Draft
   │
Technical Review
   │
Engineering Approval
   │
Published
   │
Periodic Review
   │
Revised
   │
Archived
```

Superseded knowledge shall remain available for historical project traceability.

---

# 21. Cross-Discipline Workflow Framework

Engineering disciplines shall collaborate through standardized workflows.

The objective is to minimize duplicate work while maintaining discipline ownership.

## 21.2 Shared Coordination Objects

Disciplines shall coordinate using shared engineering objects rather than duplicate information.

Examples include:

- Project
- Building
- Floor
- Zone
- Room
- Equipment
- Utility Connection
- Asset
- Drawing
- Specification
- Issue
- Inspection

Ownership of the object remains with the designated module while access is controlled through permissions.

# 22. Discipline Configuration

Each engineering discipline shall support configurable operational settings.

Examples include:

### Mechanical Engineering

- preferred pressure units;
- preferred temperature units;
- default pipe roughness;
- preferred pump manufacturers.

---

### Electrical Engineering

- default system voltage;
- preferred conductor material;
- default frequency;
- default cable insulation.

---

### Fire Protection Engineering

- preferred hazard classifications;
- default design density;
- fire water safety factors;
- preferred sprinkler types.

---

### Plumbing Engineering

- default fixture units;
- water demand methodology;
- preferred pipe materials;
- drainage standards.

Configuration shall be managed through organizational settings rather than hard-coded application logic.

# 23. Future Discipline Expansion Strategy

FEIP is designed to accommodate additional engineering disciplines without requiring fundamental architectural changes.

Potential future disciplines include:

- Environmental Engineering
- Chemical Engineering
- Mining Engineering
- Petroleum Engineering
- Aerospace Engineering
- Railway Engineering
- Agricultural Engineering
- Biomedical Engineering
- Industrial Engineering
- Telecommunications Engineering
- Geotechnical Engineering
- Process Engineering

Each new discipline shall:

- integrate with CORE;
- use shared engineering objects;
- comply with FEIP governance;
- expose standardized APIs;
- support the common reporting framework.

---

# 24. Capability Maturity Model

Engineering disciplines shall evolve through defined capability levels.

| Level | Description |
|--------|-------------|
| Level 1 | Basic engineering calculations |
| Level 2 | Engineering reports and documentation |
| Level 3 | Workflow integration |
| Level 4 | Cross-discipline collaboration |
| Level 5 | Optimization and analytics |
| Level 6 | Asset lifecycle integration |
| Level 7 | Engineering intelligence integration |

This maturity model provides a roadmap for progressive enhancement while preserving architectural consistency.

---

# 25. Design Principles for New Disciplines

Every newly introduced engineering discipline shall comply with the following principles:

1. Reuse shared CORE services.
2. Avoid duplication of existing calculations.
3. Use standardized engineering terminology where applicable.
4. Integrate with the shared project information model.
5. Implement version-controlled calculation libraries.
6. Reference centralized standards.
7. Participate in multidisciplinary workflows.
8. Support common reporting formats.
9. Maintain auditability.
10. Preserve backward compatibility whenever practical.
