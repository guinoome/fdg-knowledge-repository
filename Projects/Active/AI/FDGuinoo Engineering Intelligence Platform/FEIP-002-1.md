# 1. Purpose

This document defines the CORE Integrated Engineering Framework, the primary engineering operating model of FEIP.

CORE is not a marketing concept or artificial intelligence feature. It is the architectural and operational framework governing how engineering knowledge, calculations, optimization, compliance, and engineering intelligence interact throughout the platform.

Every engineering module within FEIP shall be implemented using one or more CORE engines.

No engineering functionality shall exist outside the CORE framework unless approved through formal architecture governance.

---

# 2. Scope

This specification governs:

- Engineering Calculation Services
- Engineering Optimization Services
- Engineering Review Services
- Compliance Validation
- Engineering Intelligence Services
- Engineering Workflow Coordination
- Cross-Module Engineering Communication
- Engineering Knowledge Retrieval
- Engineering Recommendations
- Engineering Report Generation

The framework applies to both:

- FEIP Platform (Subscriber Platform)
- FEIP Studio (Private Engineering Platform)

# 3. Framework Overview

CORE represents four specialized engineering engines operating together under a unified architecture.

```
                    CORE Integrated Engineering

               +------------------------------------+
               |        Engineering Workflow        |
               +------------------------------------+

     +-------------+-------------+--------------+-------------+
     |             |             |              |             |
     | Calculation | Optimization| Review &     | Engineering |
     |   Engine    |   Engine    | Compliance   | Intelligence|
     |             |             |    Engine    |    Engine   |
     +-------------+-------------+--------------+-------------+

                ↓ Shared Engineering Knowledge Base

                ↓ Shared Standards Library

                ↓ Shared Equipment Library

                ↓ Shared Project Data

                ↓ Shared Reporting Framework
```

Each engine owns a distinct engineering responsibility while cooperating through standardized interfaces.

# 4. Design Objectives

The CORE framework shall:

- Standardize engineering logic across all modules.
- Prevent duplication of engineering functions.
- Encourage reusable engineering services.
- Improve maintainability.
- Simplify future expansion.
- Support multidisciplinary engineering workflows.
- Enable centralized standards management.
- Preserve engineering traceability.
- Facilitate engineering intelligence.
- Improve engineering consistency across the platform.

# 5. Architectural Principles

The CORE framework is founded upon the following principles.

## 5.1 Separation of Responsibility

Each engine shall own a clearly defined engineering responsibility.

Responsibilities shall not overlap unnecessarily.

Example:

Pipe Sizing

Calculation Engine

↓

Energy Consumption

Optimization Engine

↓

Code Verification

Review & Compliance Engine

↓

Engineering Recommendation

Engineering Intelligence Engine

Each engine contributes independently while preserving modularity.

---

## 5.2 Reusability

Engineering capabilities shall be implemented once and reused throughout FEIP.

Example:

Pump Head Calculation

Rather than implementing separate pump calculations inside:

- Mechanical Module
- Fire Protection Module
- Water Supply Module
- District Cooling Module

a single validated calculation service shall be maintained within the Calculation Engine and consumed by all relevant modules.

Benefits include:

- consistent engineering results;
- simplified maintenance;
- reduced validation effort;
- centralized updates.

---

## 5.3 Standardized Interfaces

Each CORE engine shall expose documented service interfaces.

Modules shall communicate using these interfaces rather than directly accessing another engine's internal implementation.

Example:

```
Mechanical Module

↓

Calculation Service

↓

Optimization Service

↓

Compliance Service

↓

Engineering Intelligence Service

↓

Engineering Report
```

This architecture supports independent development and testing.

---

## 5.4 Traceability

Every engineering decision shall be traceable through the CORE workflow.

Typical traceability chain:

Engineering Inputs

↓

Calculation Results

↓

Optimization Results

↓

Compliance Validation

↓

Engineering Recommendation

↓

Engineering Report

↓

Project Record

↓

Audit History

Users shall be able to reconstruct how engineering conclusions were produced.

# 6. CORE Workflow

Every engineering workflow shall follow a structured sequence.

```
Engineering Request

↓

Input Validation

↓

Calculation

↓

Optimization

↓

Compliance Review

↓

Engineering Intelligence

↓

Report Generation

↓

Project Integration

↓

Audit Logging
```

Individual modules may omit stages only when those stages are not applicable.

For example:

A simple unit conversion may require only:

Validation

↓

Calculation

↓

Result

Whereas a fire pump design workflow may require all CORE stages.

# 8. Engineering Knowledge Layer

The CORE framework operates on a shared engineering knowledge layer.

This layer supplies validated engineering information to every CORE engine.

Knowledge domains include:

- Engineering equations
- Material properties
- Equipment specifications
- Design criteria
- Industry terminology
- Engineering workflows
- Standards references
- Calculation methodologies
- Engineering best practices

The knowledge layer shall be managed as a controlled engineering asset and shall support versioning, review, and audit.

---

# 9. Engine Interaction Rules

To maintain architectural consistency, the following interaction rules shall apply:

1. The Calculation Engine shall not perform optimization logic.
2. The Optimization Engine shall not modify calculation formulas.
3. The Review & Compliance Engine shall not change engineering inputs.
4. The Engineering Intelligence Engine shall not alter validated calculation results.
5. All inter-engine communication shall occur through documented service contracts.
6. Shared data shall be retrieved from authoritative sources only.
7. Every engineering transaction shall be auditable.

These rules preserve clear boundaries between responsibilities and reduce unintended coupling.
