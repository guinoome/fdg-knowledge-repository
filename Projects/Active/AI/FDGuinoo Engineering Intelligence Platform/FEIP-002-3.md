# 17. Review & Compliance Engine

## 17.1 Purpose

The Review & Compliance Engine is responsible for validating engineering work against applicable standards, organizational requirements, project criteria, and configurable engineering rules.

Unlike the Calculation Engine, which determines engineering results, the Review & Compliance Engine evaluates whether those results satisfy defined requirements.

It provides engineering validation, identifies inconsistencies, and assists engineering reviewers without modifying engineering calculations.

---

## 17.2 Objectives

The Review & Compliance Engine shall:

- Verify engineering compliance.
- Detect design inconsistencies.
- Support multidisciplinary design reviews.
- Validate engineering documentation.
- Assist engineering quality assurance.
- Improve engineering traceability.
- Reduce engineering review time.
- Standardize engineering review processes.

---

## 17.3 Responsibilities

The Review & Compliance Engine owns:

- Engineering rule validation.
- Standards verification.
- Design review checklists.
- Engineering review workflows.
- Compliance reporting.
- Engineering issue tracking.
- Review history.
- Approval recommendations.

The engine shall not:

- Perform engineering calculations.
- Modify calculation formulas.
- Change project information.
- Override engineering decisions.
- Alter approved engineering records.

# 18. Compliance Validation Framework

Engineering validation shall be organized into multiple independent layers.

```
Project Requirements
        │
        ▼
Input Validation
        │
        ▼
Engineering Standards Validation
        │
        ▼
Discipline Rules Validation
        │
        ▼
Cross-Discipline Coordination
        │
        ▼
Organizational Requirements
        │
        ▼
Compliance Report
```

Each validation layer shall execute independently and record its findings.

---

## 18.1 Validation Categories

The engine shall support validation for:

### Engineering Standards

Examples:

- Minimum pipe velocity
- Maximum pressure
- Equipment redundancy
- Safety factors
- Design limits

---

### Organizational Standards

Examples:

- Company drawing standards
- Naming conventions
- Equipment numbering
- Internal specifications
- Standard report templates

---

### Project Standards

Examples:

- Client requirements
- Project-specific design criteria
- Owner standards
- Material restrictions
- Preferred manufacturers

---

### Documentation Validation

Examples:

- Missing calculations
- Missing drawings
- Missing approvals
- Missing specifications
- Incomplete engineering reports

# 19. Engineering Review Workflow

Every engineering review shall follow a standardized lifecycle.

```
Engineering Submission

↓

Automated Validation

↓

Issue Identification

↓

Engineering Review

↓

Reviewer Comments

↓

Engineering Response

↓

Revision

↓

Final Validation

↓

Approval

↓

Audit Archive
```

Each stage shall be recorded as part of the permanent project history.

---

# 20. Compliance Issue Classification

Every detected issue shall be classified to support prioritization and reporting.

| Level | Description |
|--------|-------------|
| Critical | Public safety or regulatory non-compliance |
| Major | Significant engineering concern requiring correction |
| Moderate | Engineering inconsistency requiring review |
| Minor | Documentation or presentation issue |
| Advisory | Engineering recommendation for improvement |

Severity classification shall be configurable by administrators where organizational requirements differ.

# 21. Engineering Intelligence Engine

## 21.1 Purpose

The Engineering Intelligence Engine provides contextual engineering assistance by combining engineering knowledge, project information, user context, and outputs from other CORE engines.

Its primary objective is to improve engineering productivity through structured reasoning and knowledge retrieval.

The engine supplements engineering work but does not replace professional engineering judgment.

---

## 21.2 Objectives

The Engineering Intelligence Engine shall:

- Retrieve engineering knowledge.
- Explain engineering concepts.
- Interpret calculation results.
- Recommend engineering workflows.
- Assist report generation.
- Summarize project information.
- Support engineering investigations.
- Recommend engineering references.
- Improve engineering productivity.

---

## 21.3 Responsibilities

The Engineering Intelligence Engine owns:

- Knowledge retrieval.
- Context-aware assistance.
- Engineering explanations.
- Engineering report drafting.
- Engineering workflow guidance.
- Technical document summarization.
- Recommendation generation.

The engine shall not:

- Modify validated engineering calculations.
- Override engineering approvals.
- Certify compliance.
- Approve engineering designs.
- Alter historical engineering records.

# 24. Engine Orchestration

Engineering workflows frequently require multiple CORE engines.

An orchestration layer shall coordinate engine execution while preserving engine independence.

Typical workflow:

```
User Request
      │
      ▼
Calculation Engine
      │
      ▼
Optimization Engine
      │
      ▼
Review & Compliance Engine
      │
      ▼
Engineering Intelligence Engine
      │
      ▼
Reporting Framework
      │
      ▼
Project Module
```

The orchestration layer manages execution order, error handling, and data exchange without embedding business logic within individual engines.

---

# 25. Service Contracts

Every CORE engine shall expose documented service contracts.

Each service contract shall define:

- Service name.
- Purpose.
- Input schema.
- Output schema.
- Validation requirements.
- Authentication requirements.
- Authorization requirements.
- Error responses.
- Version identifier.
- Deprecation policy.

Service contracts shall remain backward compatible whenever practical.

---
