# WP-02 — Motion-First Experience Evolution

New governing rule:

> **Motion graphics / motion design is the default product direction. Static-template treatment is the fallback, not the target.**

A template should not merely have an animated button or fade-in.

The invitation should have an identifiable **motion concept**.

For example:

`Reveal → Transition → Story → Interaction → CTA`

That is the standard we will use to judge the 50.

# WP-02.4 — Motion-first transformation

I recommend that we now add a **Motion Requirement Score** to the master matrix.

Every design receives:

**M0 — Static**

Only allowed as fallback/print.

**M1 — Ambient Motion**

Subtle continuous movement.

**M2 — Structured Motion**

Multiple coordinated animated elements.

**M3 — Motion Design**

The design itself unfolds through choreography.

**M4 — Cinematic Experience**

The invitation behaves like a short interactive motion piece.

For ML-DEP's new direction:

> **Customer-facing digital invitations should target M2–M4.**

M0 should not be a normal digital product tier.

This is an important product distinction.

A static invitation can still exist as a **print companion**, but the digital product should justify itself through motion and interaction.

# WP-02.5 — The experience signature

I am also adding a mandatory field to every design.

Each design must answer:

> **Why would someone remember this invitation?**

Examples:

**Capiz Window**

> The invitation appears to unfold through illuminated capiz panels.

**Neon Eighteen**

> The debut invitation feels like entering a live nightlife event.

**Class of Then**

> Guests move through a visual timeline from old photographs to current memories.

**Sealed with Yes**

> The invitation begins as a sealed engagement announcement and opens into the couple's story.

**Fiesta Banderitas**

> The invitation behaves like a living festival poster.

**Product Launch**

> The invitation reveals the product like a launch campaign.

This field is critical.

If a proposed design cannot answer this question, it probably isn't differentiated enough.
