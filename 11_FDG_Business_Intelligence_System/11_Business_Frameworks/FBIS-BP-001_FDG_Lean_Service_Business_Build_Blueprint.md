# FDG Lean Service Business Build Blueprint

> **Knowledge path:** [[11_FDG_Business_Intelligence_System/11_Business_Frameworks/11_Business_Frameworks_Master_Index|Business Frameworks]] → this blueprint.

## 1. Purpose and decision boundary

Convert the supplied transcript into reusable requirements for future FDG-powered business and engineering-service builds. Preserve the useful integrated client journey while replacing personal chat memory, tool dependency, unsupported claims, and uncontrolled automation with organizational records, evidence, authority, and measurable delivery.

This is an additive Draft blueprint, not approval of a new system mother, deployment, external messaging, or revised organizational baseline. Existing approved standards prevail. The user authorized analysis, enhancement, and repository addition; formal baseline approval remains separate.

The objective is repeatable capability with a small team or an individual operator. Headcount is a design condition, not a target that overrides competence, capacity, safety, or continuity. A system that survives provider replacement but fails when its founder is unavailable is still fragile.

## 4. Six functions, one governed engagement

| Function | Enhanced FDG behavior | Required output and gate |
|---|---|---|
| Attract | Repurpose verified expertise, real demonstrations, and approved case studies; test channels rather than assuming content always wins. | Content record with sources, approved claims, publication authorization, and attribution. |
| Qualify | Offer accessible inquiry and optional booking; assess fit, prerequisites, budget range, capacity, competence, and risks. | Opportunity record, qualification reason, owner, and next action. Unqualified leads may be referred, nurtured, or closed with a reason. |
| Propose | Extract requirements from consented notes; distinguish requests, assumptions, missing information, and actual commitments. | Versioned scope, exclusions, pricing basis, milestones, acceptance criteria, and approval before release. |
| Onboard and deliver | Establish agreement, required access, readiness checks, work packages, evidence, verification, and client acceptance. | Engagement/project references, approved delivery plan, evidence register, controlled handover. |
| Administer | Prepare correspondence, reminders, billing inputs, and reconciliation exceptions using authoritative records. | Draft communication, financial reference, due task, or exception; permissions determine execution. |
| Direct and learn | Project current source records into an executive Attention Center and feed validated lessons back into knowledge governance. | Evidence-linked exception, impact, accountable owner, recommended action, deadline, freshness, and resolution record. |

Content production can use FDG Creative Studio as a presentation capability where appropriate; it does not own commercial commitments or service acceptance.

## 6. Logical architecture and ownership

Proposed implementation boundaries, subject to current approved interfaces:

- FBIS: commercial opportunity, proposal status, follow-up, pricing authorization, and enterprise financial interfaces.
- FSvIS: service definition, delivery method, quality criteria, economics, reusable service components.
- FPJIS: engagement-specific execution, milestones, work packages, project changes, and handover.
- FEIS / FBPOIS: applicable technical or operational methods, calculations, observations, and evidence.
- FDG CORE: contextual retrieval, evidence/provenance, review, and decision mechanisms.
- Integration Hub: exchange contracts and reconciliation; FWAIS: permitted scheduling and workflow execution.
- FLIS: applicable legal/contractual authority; FSIS: access, isolation, and protection requirements; FAIS: independent assurance.
- FPIS: interface and platform evolution; FMCIS: collaborator ownership and conflicts.

A dashboard is a view over these authorities. It does not silently become their record owner. Shared terminology is not permission to integrate systems.

Local-First, Offline-Capable, Agent-Neutral, Provider-Replaceable are required design constraints. Capture and drafting should work locally when external services are unavailable. Sending messages, cloud recording, booking, payments, and remote verification depend on connectivity and must expose pending or failed states. Preserve a portable export and documented restore procedure; provider outage must not block access to owned records.

## 7. Minimum data contract

Reuse canonical entity schemas where they exist. The following logical fields are requirements to map, not competing schemas.

| Record | Minimum information |
|---|---|
| Client/organization | Stable ID, tenant, contacts, responsible owner, communication/consent preferences, lifecycle state. |
| Opportunity | Client ID, service reference, source, qualified/disqualified reasons, requirements, capacity check, next action. |
| Evidence | Stable ID, source, capture date, author/speaker where available, locator/timestamp, access class, verification state. |
| Proposal | Opportunity ID, revision, scope, exclusions, assumptions, dependencies, currency, pricing-rule version, milestones, acceptance, approver, approval time. |
| Engagement | Accepted proposal revision, agreement reference, responsible delivery owner, readiness, work packages, project reference, change register. |
| Task/communication | Record reference, owner, due date/timezone, draft/approved/sent state, recipient validation, external receipt where available. |
| Financial reference | Approved amount/currency, finance-system reference, issued/paid/reconciled states, authoritative owner. |
| Lesson/template | Originating evidence, context of applicability, reusable rule, validation, reviewer, approval and version. |

Common controls: record ID, tenant ID, schema version, source system, author, created/updated time, revision, evidence links, status, and applicable access policy.

Context packet for a new agent: current approved standards and versions; client/engagement IDs; confirmed facts; unresolved questions; constraints; accepted proposal revision; permitted actions; active work-package owner; evidence references; last synchronization and next action. Load relevant material only. Keep private client data in protected operational storage; the public knowledge repository contains reusable sanitized knowledge.

## 8. Lifecycle and execution safeguards

Commercial progression: inquiry → qualified → discovery complete → proposal draft → reviewed → approved for release → released → accepted or lost/on hold.

Acceptance of a proposal is evidence of commercial progression, not proof of signed agreement, payment, technical readiness, completed delivery, or client acceptance. Track those states separately. Execution requires the prerequisites defined by the approved service and agreement. Changes after scope acceptance create a versioned change request and applicable reapproval.

Proposed event envelope: event ID, tenant ID, record ID, record revision, event type, timestamp/timezone, source, actor, evidence references, correlation ID, schema version. Use idempotency keys for retries; duplicate processing must not issue duplicate invoices or messages. Unknown external outcomes enter reconciliation rather than blind resend.

Offline writes retain local IDs and revision ancestry. Concurrent incompatible edits enter **Conflict — Review Required**. Retain both versions and route to the designated authority. Display source freshness and synchronization failures.

Transcripts, websites, and emails are evidence inputs, not executable instructions. Embedded instructions cannot expand permissions, alter approved prices, expose secrets, or authorize sending.

## 9. Automation authority and founder continuity

Agents may retrieve permitted context, extract candidates, identify gaps, calculate using approved rules, prepare drafts, and flag exceptions. External publication, communications, agreement execution, pricing commitments, payments, deletion, and authority changes follow explicit delegated permissions and required gates.

Separate draft generation, approval, and execution. An approval invalidates when a material scope, price, recipient, or term changes. Audit who approved which exact revision and what was executed.

Nex is the orchestrator of specialist agents. Use specialist work only where scope or review value justifies it; do not force multi-agent development from day one. Agents must not rewrite, refactor, replace, or take over another collaborator’s assigned work without explicit authorization. Surface overlaps through handover.

Reduce founder bottlenecks through service catalogs, preapproved pricing boundaries, bounded delegation, exception routing, and tested handovers. Provide an authorized substitute operator with documented access and recovery procedures.

## 10. Pilot and three horizons

**Now:** choose one approved service and one client journey. Start with structured records, local capture, manually reviewed proposals, tasks, and an evidence-linked dashboard. Validate the delivery backbone before expanding lead generation.

**Next:** after measured reliability, connect adapters for transcription, booking, correspondence, signatures, and finance; add a durable queue and reconciliation. Evaluate alternatives using accuracy, confidentiality, cost, portability, and recovery tests.

**Redesigned constraints:** evolve toward configurable service packages and an engagement compiler that assembles approved scope, deliverables, evidence requirements, cost rules, workflows, and handover from structured intake. Client-held credentials and portable context allow provider substitution. Cross-engagement learning uses sanitized, permissioned evidence; clients never gain access to another tenant’s data. This is a research direction, not an implemented capability.

Start with one real pilot, measure, refine, integrate, standardize, automate, then scale. Do not use arbitrary revenue or headcount aspirations as acceptance criteria.

## 11. Measures and acceptance tests

Measure proposal turnaround from complete discovery to approved release; correction/rework rate; follow-up completion; conversion by qualified cohort; delivery timeliness; evidence completeness; forecast versus actual margin; unpaid aging from finance records; founder hours per engagement; context-recovery time; failed/duplicate actions.

Declare denominators, cohort dates, exclusions, and source freshness. Compare against a documented pre-pilot baseline. Revenue, impressions, and generated-document counts alone do not establish delivery quality.

| Test | Pass condition |
|---|---|
| Missing price or scope | Mark unknown; request required input; no invented commitment. |
| Contradictory meeting statements | Retain evidence references and flag conflict for review. |
| Unapproved proposal | Release is blocked by the configured authority gate. |
| Material edit after approval | Previous approval cannot authorize the changed revision. |
| Provider replacement | Exported records and context reconstruct the engagement without inaccessible chat history. |
| Offline capture and restart | Records survive restart; pending external actions remain visibly pending. |
| Duplicate event / uncertain send | At most one confirmed external action; uncertainty enters reconciliation. |
| Concurrent edits | Both versions retained; Conflict — Review Required raised. |
| Tenant isolation | Unauthorized cross-client retrieval denied, including prompts and attachments. |
| Stale or disconnected source | Dashboard displays freshness and failure; no fabricated “live” state. |
| Lost opportunity | Closure reason and retention state preserved; no automatic destructive cleanup. |
| New operator handover | Operator identifies completed/pending work, reasons, decisions, evidence, standards, and next actions. |
| Delivery completion | Required verification and acceptance evidence exist; open exceptions remain visible. |
| Learning promotion | Candidate lesson remains unapproved until the governing review/approval process completes. |

## 12. Future build handover

Before coding: retrieve current baseline; inspect existing entities, authentication, access policies, approved service, interfaces, and active work packages; identify reuse/extension and unresolved authority.

Work package must name accountable owner, bounded scope, dependencies, permitted writes, data classification, pilot records, acceptance tests, and rollback/recovery approach. Deliver working capture-to-handover flow with real persistence and clearly labeled demo data. Report what passed, what remains unverified, and operational limits.

Do not claim readiness from screenshots or an attractive artifact. Demonstrate at least one persisted journey, one failed integration, one conflict, provider-independent export/restore, and authorized operator handover.
