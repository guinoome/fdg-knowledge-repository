# Principle

Recurring revenue is useful only when its **quality** is understood.

Subscription revenue can still be weak if:
- churn is high;
- gross margin is low;
- one customer dominates;
- support burden is large;
- founder intervention is routine;
- collections are unreliable;
- security/compliance liabilities are disproportionate;
- the product requires continuous custom work.

## Minimum recurring-revenue metrics

- Monthly Recurring Revenue (MRR)
- Annual Recurring Revenue (ARR)
- subscriber/customer growth
- gross churn
- revenue churn
- gross revenue retention (GRR)
- net revenue retention (NRR)
- average revenue per user/account (ARPU)
- customer lifetime value (LTV)
- customer acquisition cost (CAC)
- CAC payback period
- gross margin
- customer concentration
- expansion/contraction MRR
- failed-payment/recovery rate

MRR/ARR are management metrics, not substitutes for accounting revenue.

## Platform test

A platform is not automatically valuable because it has many users. A network effect exists only when the value to a participant genuinely changes with participation by other users/sides.

Therefore classify each FDG platform:

- no meaningful network effect;
- data/learning effect;
- same-side network effect;
- cross-side network effect;
- ecosystem/complementor effect.

Do not assume network effects in forecasts without evidence.

## FDG recurring revenue ladder

`One-off service → Repeatable service → Retainer → Managed service → Subscription → Enterprise license → Platform/usage revenue → IP royalties`

Each step can increase leverage, but each also introduces new obligations.

## Owner-independent subscription requirement

Revenue should not count fully as owner-independent unless:
- onboarding is repeatable;
- billing/collections are governed;
- support is routable;
- service-level expectations are explicit;
- incident handling exists;
- technical operations have backups;
- knowledge is not founder-only;
- product release controls are institutionalized.
