# Purpose

This standard defines how approved engineering knowledge is preserved to ensure its long-term availability, integrity, traceability, and reuse.

Knowledge preservation protects the engineering capability of the FDG Ecosystem from loss, fragmentation, or technological change.

---

# Core Principle

Preserve engineering intelligence.

Not software.

Not platforms.

Not conversations.

Knowledge shall outlive the tools used to create it.

---

# Preservation Objectives

Knowledge Preservation shall:

- maintain engineering integrity
- ensure long-term accessibility
- support organizational continuity
- enable future reuse
- preserve historical reasoning
- remain technology-independent whenever practical

# Preservation Scope

The following shall normally be preserved.

## Governance

- constitutions
- standards
- policies
- engineering principles

---

## Engineering Decisions

- Architecture Decision Records (ADRs)
- decision rationale
- trade-off analysis
- assumptions

---

## Engineering Standards

- approved methodologies
- frameworks
- templates
- procedures

---

## Reusable Knowledge

- lessons learned
- engineering patterns
- calculation methods
- best practices
- validated workflows

---

## Organizational History

Historical versions shall remain available for engineering traceability.

History is preserved.

Not overwritten.

---

# Preservation Requirements

Every preserved knowledge asset shall have:

- unique document identifier
- version information
- approval status
- owner
- related documents
- change history

---

# Repository Requirements

Approved knowledge shall:

- exist within the FDG Knowledge Repository
- be linked through Maps of Content
- support bidirectional linking
- remain searchable
- remain discoverable

Knowledge hidden from retrieval has limited organizational value.

---

# Technology Independence

Knowledge shall be stored using formats that maximize long-term accessibility.

Where practical, prefer:

- Markdown
- Plain text
- Open standards

Repository structure shall not depend upon a single software platform.

---

# Historical Integrity

Historical knowledge shall never be deleted solely because a newer version exists.

Historical versions provide:

- engineering traceability
- decision context
- evolution history
- audit support

Superseded knowledge shall be archived, not destroyed.

---

# Preservation Checklist

Before preservation, verify:

✓ Approved status confirmed.

✓ Version assigned.

✓ Document ID assigned.

✓ Repository location established.

✓ Maps of Content updated.

✓ Related documents linked.

✓ Historical references preserved.

---

Specialized Collaborators

---

# Preservation Rules

Knowledge Preservation shall never:

- remove approved history
- replace previous reasoning
- create duplicate authoritative documents
- depend on a single collaborator
- depend on a single technology platform

---

# Organizational Principle

Execution systems manage the present.

Knowledge systems preserve the future.

Engineering capability survives because approved knowledge survives.

---

# Continuous Improvement

The preservation system shall evolve without compromising historical integrity.

Repository improvements shall strengthen accessibility while preserving authority and traceability.

---

# Governing Principle

The enduring asset of the FDG Ecosystem is not its software.

It is its governed engineering knowledge.
