# Purpose

This standard defines the operational practices required to protect FDG information, systems, collaborators, and intelligence capabilities.

---

# Core Principle

Security is not a restriction.

Security enables reliable capability.

---

# Security Objectives

Nex security shall protect:

- confidentiality
- integrity
- availability
- accountability
- traceability

---

# Security Operating Model

```
Identify

↓

Protect

↓

Control

↓

Monitor

↓

Respond

↓

Improve

```

---

# 1. Identity Management

Every user, collaborator, or system must have:

- defined identity
- assigned responsibility
- controlled access

---

# Identity Categories

## Human Identity

Examples:

- engineers
- managers
- administrators

---

## AI Collaborator Identity

Examples:

- Nex
- specialized engineering agents

---

## System Identity

Examples:

- applications
- databases
- automation services

---

# 2. Access Control

Access shall follow:

```
Need

+

Role

+

Responsibility

+

Minimum Required Permission

```

---

# Least Privilege Principle

Users and systems should receive only the access required to perform assigned responsibilities.

---

# Access Levels

## Public

General information.

---

## Internal

FDG operational information.

---

## Restricted

Sensitive engineering or business information.

---

## Confidential

Critical strategic information.

---

# 3. Information Protection

Information should be protected according to:

- sensitivity
- ownership
- business impact
- operational impact

---

# Protected Information Examples

## Engineering Information

- designs
- calculations
- specifications

---

## Business Information

- financial data
- customer information
- strategies

---

## System Information

- configurations
- credentials
- architecture details

---

# 4. AI Trust Boundary

AI collaborators shall have:

## Defined Purpose

Why they exist.

---

## Defined Context

What information they may access.

---

## Defined Authority

What actions they may perform.

---

## Defined Validation

How outputs are checked.

---

# AI Autonomy Control

Autonomy should progress:

```
Suggestion

↓

Assistance

↓

Controlled Execution

↓

Limited Automation

↓

Optimization

```

---

# 5. Auditability

Important actions should be traceable.

Records may include:

- user
- system
- action
- date
- result

---

# 6. Data Protection

Data handling should consider:

- storage location
- backup
- transfer
- retention
- disposal

---

# 7. Backup and Recovery

Critical knowledge should have:

- backup strategy
- recovery process
- ownership

---

# Recovery Principle

A system is not reliable unless it can recover.

---

# 8. Security Monitoring

Monitor for:

- unauthorized access
- abnormal behavior
- data integrity issues
- system failures

---

# Incident Response

When security events occur:

```
Detect

↓

Contain

↓

Investigate

↓

Recover

↓

Improve

```

---

# Trust Evaluation

Before adopting a new technology:

Evaluate:

## Security

Can information be protected?

---

## Reliability

Can it be trusted operationally?

---

## Ownership

Who controls the capability?

---

## Exit Strategy

Can FDG migrate if needed?

---

# Anti-Pattern Prevention

## Excessive Access

Giving convenience over control.

---

## Invisible Automation

Systems acting without traceability.

---

## Vendor Dependency

Losing control of critical capability.

---

## Security Afterthought

Adding protection after deployment.

---

Approves critical access.

---

## Nex

Maintains trust framework.

Identifies security risks.

---

## Collaborators

Follow access requirements.

Protect information.

---

# Governing Principle

FDG intelligence must always remain:

Controlled.

Explainable.

Auditable.

Trustworthy.
