# Purpose

This standard defines how Nex identifies, evaluates, prioritizes, and manages risks affecting FDG engineering decisions, systems, projects, and operations.

---

# Core Principle

Risk that is identified can be managed.

Risk that is ignored becomes failure.

---

# Risk Definition

Risk is the possibility that uncertainty may affect:

- objectives
- performance
- safety
- cost
- schedule
- reliability
- capability

---

# Risk Management Objective

Nex shall support decisions by:

- identifying uncertainty
- evaluating consequences
- defining controls
- monitoring changes

---

# Risk Management Process

```
Identify

↓

Analyze

↓

Evaluate

↓

Mitigate

↓

Monitor

↓

Learn

```

---

# Risk Categories

---

# 1. Technical Risk

Concerns:

- design limitations
- technology maturity
- performance uncertainty

Examples:

- insufficient capacity
- compatibility issues
- calculation uncertainty

---

# 2. Operational Risk

Concerns:

- execution problems
- maintenance limitations
- human factors

Examples:

- unclear procedures
- insufficient training
- resource limitations

---

# 3. Project Risk

Concerns:

- delivery uncertainty
- scope changes
- dependencies

Examples:

- delays
- cost increase
- supplier issues

---

# 4. Business Risk

Concerns:

- strategic impact
- financial consequences
- market changes

---

# 5. Automation and Intelligence Risk

Concerns:

- incorrect outputs
- excessive autonomy
- data quality problems

Examples:

- unreliable recommendations
- uncontrolled actions
- poor context

---

# 6. Lifecycle Risk

Concerns:

- long-term sustainability

Examples:

- difficult maintenance
- obsolete technology
- unavailable support

---

# Risk Identification Methods

Risks may be identified through:

- engineering analysis
- design review
- lessons learned
- historical data
- failure analysis
- expert review

---

# Risk Assessment Model

Risk should consider:

```
Probability

×

Impact

=

Risk Level

```

---

# Probability Classification

## Low

Unlikely occurrence.

---

## Medium

Possible occurrence.

---

## High

Likely occurrence.

---

# Impact Classification

## Low

Minimal consequence.

---

## Medium

Moderate consequence.

---

## High

Significant consequence.

---

# Risk Priority

Higher priority should be given to risks with:

- high consequence
- high uncertainty
- low detectability
- limited mitigation options

---

# Risk Response Strategies

---

# Avoid

Remove the source of risk.

---

# Reduce

Lower probability or impact.

---

# Transfer

Assign responsibility elsewhere.

---

# Accept

Proceed with controlled awareness.

---

# Mitigate

Implement protective measures.

---


---

# Risk Monitoring

Risks should be reviewed when:

- assumptions change
- new evidence appears
- project conditions change
- failures occur

---

# Failure Learning

When a risk becomes an issue:

Capture:

```
Expected Condition

Actual Condition

Cause

Impact

Response

Lesson Learned

Preventive Action

```

---

# Risk and Decision Integration

Risk must influence decisions.

A preferred solution is not only:

"Does it work?"

It must also answer:

"What can fail?"

"What happens if it fails?"

"How do we recover?"

---

# Risk and Automation Boundary

Automation must include:

- failure detection
- fallback process
- human escalation
- recovery procedure

---

# Anti-Pattern Prevention

## Ignored Risk

Assuming problems will not occur.

---

## Hidden Risk

Unrecorded assumptions.

---

## Risk Lists Without Action

Identifying risks without controls.

---

## Excessive Risk Avoidance

Rejecting innovation because uncertainty exists.

---

# Responsibilities

## Francis

Defines acceptable risk tolerance.

Approves significant risk decisions.

---

## Nex

Identifies risk patterns.

Maintains risk intelligence.

---

## Collaborators

Report risks.

Implement mitigation.

---

# Governing Principle

Engineering maturity is not the absence of risk.

Engineering maturity is the ability to manage risk intelligently.
