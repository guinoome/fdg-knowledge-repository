# FDG AI Filmmaking and Media Production Skill

## Purpose and ownership

This is an FDG-owned, provider-neutral production skill. Nex acts as creative/production orchestrator and may assemble a dynamic FMCIS team of human specialists and AI collaborators. The FDG Knowledge Repository—not a model, platform, subscription or chat—is the durable source of approved production knowledge.

The objective is repeatable, rights-aware, technically coherent, commercially effective media at the lowest total cost per accepted deliverable. Do not optimize for impressive demos, generation volume or tool popularity.

## Mandatory context and governance

Before material work, retrieve the FMCIS Master Index, delegation-first rule, current capability registry and relevant engagement handovers; NEX-COLLAB-LEARN-001; NEX-ROLE-ENGINEERING-001; existing video source-fidelity/multimodal review guidance; and applicable project owner, brand, privacy, legal/IP, security and FPJIS acceptance requirements.

Nex remains orchestrator; founder retains final authority. Delegate bounded specialist work when an actual dispatch mechanism is available. If it is not, report DISPATCH BLOCKED and provide a ready-to-send work package. Never pretend a collaborator was consulted or a review was completed.

## Dynamic production team

Select only roles needed for the task:
1. Creative director / producer — objective, audience, concept, narrative, deliverable and budget.
2. Screenwriter / story editor — script, hook, beat sheet, dialogue, CTA and claims.
3. Director of photography / cinematographer — shot design, lens, camera movement, composition, lighting, exposure, continuity and visual language.
4. Storyboard / previs artist — shot boards, animatics, reference frames and timing.
5. Generative-media engineer — image/video model selection, reference conditioning, prompting, generation settings, consistency and failure analysis.
6. Production designer / art director — locations, props, palette, wardrobe, product depiction and brand system.
7. Editor / post-production specialist — selects, pacing, continuity, transitions, titles, captions, color and delivery.
8. Sound designer / composer / voice specialist — dialogue, narration, ambience, Foley, music, mix and loudness.
9. Marketing strategist — audience insight, offer, proof, platform-native creative, CTA and experiment design.
10. Rights / privacy reviewer — asset provenance, consent, licensing, model terms, data egress and release constraints.
11. Independent quality reviewer — checks the final artifact against the approved brief and acceptance rubric.

A person or collaborator may cover multiple roles only where competence is evidenced and review independence is preserved for material claims.

## Production lifecycle

### 1. Brief and commercial objective
Capture target customer, audience awareness, customer problem, offer, supported proof, desired action, distribution channels, duration, aspect ratios, languages, brand rules, deadlines, approval owner and budget. Separate verified product facts from assumptions and creative claims.

### 2. Research and concept
Research audience, comparable campaigns and relevant product truth. Produce a creative brief, concept alternatives, narrative arc, hook, CTA and claims register. Do not copy protected creative expression or competitor assets.

### 3. Pre-production
Produce script, beat sheet, scene list, shot list, storyboard/reference frames, timing, visual bible, character/environment/product reference sheets, camera and lighting plan, audio plan, asset manifest and generation budget. Lock critical visual references before generating many shots.

### 4. Tool/model routing
Choose a capability, not a brand. Distinguish platform/workspace (OpenArt, Higgsfield, Runway, Google Flow); model (Veo, Nano Banana, Seedance, Kling, Flux); feature/workflow (reference image, character identity, camera control, scene builder, inpainting); and editor/renderer/final delivery system.

Verify exact model identity, version, availability, input/output limits, reference behavior, commercial terms, provenance, watermarking, export, credit costs and regional/account constraints from current official sources or observed authorized tests. A product name such as Omni is ambiguous until its exact provider and feature/model identity are confirmed.

Do not require paid subscriptions. Prefer existing authorized integrations, free tiers/credits, local/open-source tools and public documentation. A free tier is not permission to upload private, client, employee, confidential or unreleased media. Never create an account, accept terms, buy credits, start a trial or incur spend without approval.

### 5. Generation and continuity
Generate representative reference frames and short proof shots before scaling. Maintain a visual bible and continuity ledger for character identity, face/body presentation, wardrobe/accessories; product geometry, logo, colors, labels, controls and safety features; location layout, props, weather and time; camera axis, screen direction, lens, framing, motion and lighting; scene chronology, action state and audio perspective.

Use consistent references, seeds where supported, shot-specific prompts and controlled variation. Do not assume a reference feature guarantees identity or product fidelity. Inspect every selected output. Regenerate or edit failures rather than hiding them in the final cut.

### 6. Editing and finishing
Assemble only approved shots. Check screen direction, eyelines, geography, action continuity, temporal consistency, object permanence, exposure, color, motion artifacts, typography, spelling, logo fidelity, audio sync, voice intelligibility, music rights, captions, pacing and CTA visibility. Preserve editable project files and source/output manifests where available.

### 7. Quality, rights and release
Use a written acceptance rubric tied to the brief. Review the complete video, not isolated showcase frames. Verify asset/model terms and licenses, human likeness/voice consent, music/font/stock rights, privacy and client approvals. Do not publish, send to customers or claim campaign performance without explicit authority and evidence.

### 8. Learning and knowledge return
Capture reusable findings as proposed provider-neutral skills or provider-specific adapter skills. Follow NEX-COLLAB-LEARN-001: compare existing knowledge, cite sources, preserve provenance, test positive and negative cases, independent review, version and rollback. Never silently promote a proposal or claim base-model training.

## Required deliverables

For a complete production engagement, return:
- approved creative brief and claims register;
- script, beat sheet, shot list and storyboard/reference set;
- visual bible and continuity ledger;
- model/tool routing decision with source links and observed configuration;
- generation log including failures, revisions, credits/cost and selected takes;
- editable timeline/project and asset provenance manifest where available;
- final exports and platform variants;
- acceptance checklist, reviewer findings and owner approval;
- actual production cost, elapsed time, accepted duration and cost per accepted finished minute;
- concise handover and proposed repository learning.

If a deliverable was not created or a test was not run, state NOT CREATED / NOT RUN.

## Benchmark method

Compare tools on identical briefs, reference assets, constraints, model versions and evaluation rubrics. Record sample size and failed generations. Measure visual quality and cinematographic coherence; prompt/reference adherence; character, product and environment consistency; controllability and editability; usable-shot yield and first-pass acceptance; audio quality and synchronization; rights/privacy and export constraints; latency, operator time, credit/cash cost and rework.

Primary unit economics:
- cost per approved shot;
- cost per approved finished minute;
- human review/edit hours per finished minute;
- first-pass acceptance and usable-shot yield.

Include free-tier opportunity cost, failed generations, rework and collaborator time. Do not compare headline subscription prices alone. Do not generalize from one showcase generation.

## Safety and engineering truth

AI-generated engineering scenes are illustrative unless independently checked against approved technical source material. Never depict invented specifications, unsafe installation practices, false performance claims, fabricated certifications, or misleading savings. Do not use generated imagery as engineering evidence, simulation output or proof of installed conditions.

## Status discipline

Use explicit states: PROPOSED, RESEARCHED, SOURCE-VERIFIED, TESTED, INDEPENDENTLY-REVIEWED, APPROVED, DEPLOYED, BLOCKED. Documentation is not execution; a model feature description is not a successful test; a rendered demo is not client acceptance or revenue.
