### Review objectives

1. Obtain lawful access to the webinar and review the complete material. Do not bypass access controls, paywalls or account restrictions.
2. Produce a timestamped breakdown of the actual editing workflow and techniques demonstrated.
3. Identify the intended ad format and narrative mechanics, including any micro-drama/story-led structure, hook, tension, reveal, proof, offer and CTA. Treat “micro drama” as campaign metadata until confirmed by the video itself.
4. Analyze practical editing decisions:
   - opening hook and first 1–3 seconds
   - shot duration, pacing, rhythm and pattern interrupts
   - continuity, coverage, shot/reverse-shot and match cuts
   - transitions and their narrative purpose
   - captions, kinetic typography, supers and safe-area placement
   - dialogue, voiceover, ambience, Foley, music and sound effects
   - color, exposure matching and visual hierarchy
   - aspect-ratio adaptation, especially 9:16 mobile-first delivery
   - B-roll, inserts, product proof and CTA sequencing
   - versioning, creative testing and production throughput
5. Separate techniques actually shown from techniques merely described or implied.
6. Record the editing application, plugins, templates, AI tools and model versions only when explicitly identified and verifiable.
7. Evaluate whether each method is transferable across editing platforms and can be expressed as an FDG-owned SOP or skill.
8. Compare the workflow against the existing FDG media production lifecycle and editing/post-production capability. Identify gaps, duplication and opportunities to REUSE / EXTEND / MERGE / REPLACE.
9. Recommend a small practical exercise to reproduce the transferable editing method using FDG-approved or synthetic/public-rights assets, with measurable acceptance criteria.
10. Identify any commercial claims, earnings claims, testimonials, guarantees or pricing statements. Record them as vendor claims, not established facts, unless independently substantiated.
