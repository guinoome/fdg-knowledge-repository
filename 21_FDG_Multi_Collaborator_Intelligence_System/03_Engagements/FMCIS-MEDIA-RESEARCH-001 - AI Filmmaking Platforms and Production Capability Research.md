# FMCIS-MEDIA-RESEARCH-001 — AI Filmmaking Platforms, Models and Production Capability Research

## Founder direction

Develop Nex and the FDG collaborator team into a durable AI-assisted filmmaking and commercial media-production capability. Nex may request expertise from the appropriate FMCIS collaborators; collaborators are expected to research, challenge, test and teach—not merely return promotional summaries.

**No subscription purchase is required for this research or capability development.** Do not assume FDG must subscribe to OpenArt, Higgsfield, Runway, Google Flow or any other platform. First use public official documentation, existing authorized integrations, available free access/credits, open-source implementations and controlled synthetic test assets. Any account creation, paid plan, credit purchase, trial, terms acceptance or external data transfer requires the applicable approval.

## Architectural disposition

**EXTEND** existing FMCIS capability coordination, NEX-COLLAB-LEARN-001 controlled learning, NEX-ROLE-ENGINEERING-001 persistent role engineering, existing video source-fidelity review and FDG Creative Studio direction.

Do not create a new intelligence system, subscription stack, duplicate agent organization or vendor-owned knowledge base. This work package commissions a domain capability and reusable skill set under FMCIS; it does not mark FMCIS-001–008 complete.

## Research scope

Investigate the platform, model, workflow and production ecosystem, including but not limited to:

- OpenArt — multi-model creative workspace, character/asset consistency, storyboard and multi-scene workflows, integrations/agent interfaces, editing and export.
- Higgsfield — Cinema Studio, camera/lens controls, Omni (exact product/model/feature identity to be resolved), Soul ID/identity consistency, advertising/Marketing Studio and model routing.
- Google Flow — Veo model access, reference/ingredient conditioning, frames-to-video, scene building, extension, audio, export and free access limits.
- Nano Banana — exact Google model family/version and supported image-editing/reference capabilities; distinguish model from Flow and other Google products.
- Runway — generation, reference conditioning, editing, Aleph or successor tools, timeline/export and API/connector capabilities.
- Other relevant tools/models discovered by specialists, including Seedance, Kling, Luma, Flux, ComfyUI and open-source/local workflows where relevant.

Do not assume these named products are equivalent categories. Maintain separate records for platform, model, feature, adapter and production workflow. Resolve ambiguous names such as Omni before making claims.

## FMCIS specialist assignments

Nex/FMCIS should assemble a capability-weighted, dynamic team. Suggested work packages:

1. **Platform intelligence researcher:** official feature matrix, product boundaries, model catalog, integrations, availability, limits, export and current pricing/credit mechanics.
2. **Filmmaking director / cinematographer:** shot control, camera/lens language, lighting, motion, scene continuity, visual storytelling and limitations of generated footage.
3. **Generative-media engineer:** reference conditioning, character/product/environment consistency, image-to-video, start/end frames, prompt controls, seeds, motion, reproducibility and failure modes.
4. **Editor / post-production specialist:** shot selection, timeline, pacing, continuity repair, color, captions, sound, voice, music and final export.
5. **Commercial producer / marketing strategist:** product ads, direct response, UGC, corporate film, hooks, audience, offer, CTA, platform variants and conversion measurement.
6. **Rights/privacy/security reviewer:** model and asset terms, commercial-use rights, watermarking, likeness/voice consent, source provenance, data retention, egress and client-data restrictions.
7. **Independent red-team reviewer:** challenge unsupported claims, vendor lock-in, benchmark bias, hidden costs, poor repeatability and missing acceptance tests.

Use human specialists and AI collaborators where actual expertise and availability are established. Record requested versus actually dispatched collaborators. A collaborator's promotional or model-generated confidence is not evidence.

## Research method and required evidence

1. Confirm exact legal product identity, provider, model/version and date.
2. Read current official documentation, pricing/credit pages, terms, commercial-use/IP policy, privacy/data handling and API/MCP documentation where available.
3. Inspect open-source repositories and alternatives where they address the same capability. Check license, maintenance, security, dependencies, portability and operating burden.
4. Separate documented claims, directly observed behavior, independent tests, inference and unknowns.
5. Record free-tier access and constraints without assuming it is available in every region/account.
6. Test only through existing authorized access or genuinely free access. Use synthetic or FDG-approved non-sensitive assets. No confidential/client/employee footage or unreleased product data to cloud services without explicit data-owner approval.
7. Capture screenshots or output artifact references, exact settings, model/version, date, prompt/reference inputs, duration/resolution, credit usage, failures and reviewer notes when a test is actually performed.
8. Compare identical tasks and acceptance rubrics. Include failed attempts, rework and human operator time.
9. Provide a recommendation and disposition: REUSE / EXTEND / MERGE / REPLACE / NEW, plus NOT ADOPTED / UNVERIFIED / BLOCKED where evidence is insufficient.

## Benchmark tasks

Use the same approved synthetic or public-rights test pack across candidates:
- one product still image with text/logo and geometry that must remain accurate;
- one character/host across at least three scenes;
- one environment across day/night or camera-angle changes;
- one 4–6-shot 20–30 second commercial sequence;
- one targeted edit to an existing shot while preserving non-target content;
- one narration/music/caption finishing task.

Measure prompt/reference adherence, product and identity consistency, camera controllability, temporal artifacts, usable-shot yield, first-pass acceptance, editability, export quality, rights/privacy, latency, credits/cash cost, human review/edit time and cost per accepted finished minute. Record sample size; a single generation is a demonstration, not a benchmark.

## Knowledge products to create

The durable outcome is a set of FDG-owned, provider-neutral and provider-specific skills—not a list of subscriptions.

Initial proposed skill family:
- fdg-ai-filmmaking — master production lifecycle and role coordination.
- fdg-cinematic-direction — script, story, cinematography, shot design and visual continuity.
- fdg-generative-media-engineering — image/video model routing, reference conditioning and consistency.
- fdg-ai-video-postproduction — edit, sound, color, captions and delivery.
- fdg-commercial-video-production — marketing objectives, ad formats, claims and performance.
- Provider adapters (e.g. OpenArt, Higgsfield, Google Flow/Veo, Nano Banana, Runway) only after each is independently researched and validated.

Provider-specific skill content must document exact supported capabilities, limitations, version/date, source links, task routing, prompt/input contracts, data boundaries, cost model, fallback and tested examples. Do not copy upstream skills wholesale or install third-party skills globally. The FDG-owned skill defines the organizational procedure; an adapter translates that procedure to a provider's verified interface.

## Acceptance and promotion gates

- MR-001 Identity: ambiguous product/model names resolved with official evidence.
- MR-002 Source traceability: every material capability, price, rights and limitation claim has a current source and retrieval date.
- MR-003 Category separation: platform, model, feature, workflow and adapter are not conflated.
- MR-004 Independent review: a specialist other than the primary researcher challenges findings; disagreements are retained and arbitrated under FMCIS.
- MR-005 Repeatability: controlled test tasks, settings, output references, failures and sample sizes are recorded; untested features remain UNVERIFIED.
- MR-006 Economics: cost per approved shot/minute includes failed generations, rework and human time; no subscription-price-only recommendation.
- MR-007 Rights and privacy: commercial terms, asset rights, likeness/voice consent and data egress reviewed before any client or public production.
- MR-008 Skill quality: proposed skills are portable, scoped, versioned, source-backed, tested, reversible and reviewed under NEX-COLLAB-LEARN-001.
- MR-009 Capability training: role contracts, approved exemplars, exercises and evaluation rubrics exist; no claim that an underlying model was retrained.
- MR-010 Business acceptance: at least one complete FDG-owned sample production passes the brief and review rubric before claiming an operational production capability.

## First commercial pilot

Use FDG Solar Visayas only after the product owner approves the brief and source assets. A candidate 20–30 second commercial should use accurate FDG product imagery, verified claims, a defined customer audience and clear CTA. Begin with storyboard/reference frames and a few proof shots; do not generate the entire film before visual direction and product fidelity are accepted.

The same brief can compare multiple authorized/free workflows. Paid access is not a prerequisite. No publication, customer distribution or campaign spend is authorized by this research work package.
