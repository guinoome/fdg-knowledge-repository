# FMCIS Architecture Foundation

## System Role

FMCIS converts one person's intent into coordinated multi-collaborator, multi-agent execution while preserving authority, accountability, evidence, memory, and final human approval.

## Authority

Francis is the Founder and final authority.

Nex is the FDG Architecture & Intelligence Authority.

FMCIS is a coordination and intelligence-exchange system. It does not become a governing authority above Nex.

The Founder may bypass a Nex recommendation. That decision must be recorded and surfaced to Nex as learning context.

## Dynamic Capability Teams

FMCIS forms temporary teams from capabilities required by the engagement. Example capabilities include Chief of Staff, Researcher, Architect, Domain Specialist, Coder/Engineer, Designer/Artist, Security, QA, Documentation, and Critic/Red Team.

These are capabilities, not a permanent fixed agent roster.

## Two-Level Orchestration

### FMCIS orchestration

Determines the objective, constraints, required capabilities, work packages, evidence requirements, review requirements, quality threshold, and collaborator selection.

### Collaborator-native orchestration

Claude Code, Codex, or another execution environment determines its own models, native skills, subagents, tools, and parallel execution strategy.

FMCIS should not recreate those native execution frameworks.

## Planning vs Execution

Conversational collaborators are primarily useful for independent planning, research, alternative analysis, strategic discussion, and critique.

Agent environments are primarily useful for repository work, implementation, tool use, testing, and iterative execution.

The distinction is capability-based rather than permanent.

## Model Allocation

Model selection should be based on task complexity, risk, novelty, evidence requirements, parallelizability, and available capability.

> **Do not maximize agents. Maximize useful intelligence per unit of time, token, and cost.**

## Loop Engineering

Agents do not stop merely because they produced an answer. They continue through execution, inspection, challenge, correction, testing, and re-evaluation until the defined acceptance criteria are met.

> **Every failed loop must produce information that improves the next loop.**

## Memory

FMCIS retains engagement-level memory: plans, collaborator findings, disagreements, iterations, evidence, failures, decisions, and lessons.

Not every engagement artifact becomes permanent FDG knowledge.

The future dashboard should eventually automate intake so manual copy/paste is a prototype-only method.

## Nex Availability

Nex being offline does not block FMCIS.

During the current phase, Francis reviews and approves the result. FMCIS prepares a structured package that can later be placed in a future Nex Queue.

When Nex becomes continuously available, FMCIS can pass the same structured engagement intelligence to Nex without changing the underlying workflow.
