# Mechanical Engineering Intelligence

> **FEIS — FDG Engineering Intelligence System**
>
> **Branch Constitution**
>
> **Engineering First. Intelligence Second. Technology Third.**

---

# 1. Purpose

This document establishes the official architecture, philosophy, governance, long-term vision, and development direction of the **Mechanical Engineering Intelligence** branch within the FDG Engineering Intelligence System (FEIS).

It serves as the highest-level governing document for all Mechanical Engineering Intelligence modules.

Every future Mechanical Engineering module shall conform to this specification.

---

# 2. Scope

Mechanical Engineering Intelligence encompasses the complete lifecycle of mechanical engineering activities, including:

- Engineering Analysis
- Engineering Design
- Engineering Calculation
- Engineering Validation
- Engineering Visualization
- Engineering Simulation
- Engineering Documentation
- Construction Support
- Commissioning
- Operations
- Maintenance
- Optimization
- Asset Lifecycle Management
- Engineering Knowledge Management

---

# 3. Vision

Mechanical Engineering Intelligence shall become a comprehensive engineering environment that assists engineers throughout the complete engineering lifecycle.

It is not intended to be merely a collection of calculators.

Instead, it shall integrate engineering knowledge, engineering reasoning, engineering visualization, simulation, optimization, reporting, and decision support into a unified engineering platform.

---

# 4. Mission

Develop reusable engineering intelligence capable of assisting mechanical engineers in making technically sound, evidence-based, standards-compliant decisions while maintaining full engineering transparency and traceability.

---

# 5. Engineering Philosophy

Mechanical Engineering Intelligence follows the fundamental principles of the FDG Ecosystem.

## Engineering First

Engineering decisions shall always take precedence over software implementation.

---

## Explainable Engineering

Every engineering result shall clearly explain:

- What was calculated.
- Why it was calculated.
- Which methodology was used.
- Which engineering references apply.
- How the final result was obtained.
- How the engineer should interpret the output.

No black-box engineering calculations are permitted.

---

## Evidence-Based Engineering

Engineering conclusions shall be supported by:

- Engineering standards
- Engineering references
- Mathematical validation
- Engineering assumptions
- Version-controlled formulas

---

## Visualization Before Automation

Engineers should understand the engineering before automating it.

Whenever practical, engineering visualization shall accompany engineering calculations.

---

## Continuous Improvement

Mechanical Engineering Intelligence shall evolve through engineering validation, operational experience, and continuous knowledge refinement.
