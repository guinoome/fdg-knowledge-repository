# FEIS-CM-0903 — External Product Benchmark Research and Extraction Handover

## 1. Mission

Study relevant external construction, engineering, project-controls and field-operations products deeply enough to extract useful capabilities, workflows, data relationships, commercial packaging and design lessons for FDG.

The objective is not to copy products, create feature wishlists or recommend tools based on marketing pages. It is to improve FDG through evidence-backed capability discovery.

**First worked reference:** [[08_FEIS_Engineering_Intelligence_Systems/02_Construction_Management/FEIS-CM-RSCH-0002 - ConstructPRO Product Benchmark Extraction 2026-11-19|ConstructPRO Product Benchmark Extraction]].

## 2. Required operating rules

1. Read existing FDG architecture and previous benchmark research before beginning. Reuse prior findings; do not repeat completed audits without a reason.
2. Study the source product itself: official product pages, documentation, help center, release notes, demos/videos, app marketplace, API docs, pricing/licensing, security/privacy material and credible independent user evidence where available.
3. Separate vendor claims, observed behavior, independently corroborated facts, technical inference and FDG recommendations.
4. Record exact source URLs, publication/update dates where available, access date and relevant sections. Preserve screenshots or source excerpts only where lawful and useful.
5. Never claim a feature is implemented, tested, available, included in a license or production-ready merely because a marketing page lists it.
6. Keep product identities and module boundaries distinct. Do not merge related products or attribute host-platform features to an extension without evidence.
7. Respect copyright, licenses, terms of use and trade secrets. Extract concepts and workflow lessons; do not reproduce proprietary code, protected UI assets, templates, text or database structures.
8. Apply REUSE / EXTEND / MERGE / REPLACE / NEW to each proposed FDG capability. Existing FDG architecture is the baseline; do not create parallel systems.
9. Preserve existing repository material. Make additive updates, create successor artifacts when needed, and use wikilinks.
10. No external account creation, vendor contact, purchase, paid trial, data upload or production integration without required Founder approval.

## 3. Research workflow

### Phase A — Source discovery and product identity

- Identify vendor, product, target customer, primary jobs-to-be-done, product family and host-platform dependencies.
- Separate the product from companion products, partner modules and base-platform capabilities.
- Identify deployment model, supported platforms, regions/jurisdictions and user roles where evidence exists.
- Build a source register with authority, date, scope and confidence.

### Phase B — Workflow reconstruction

Reconstruct the end-to-end user and data workflow, not just the menu of features.

For each workflow identify:
- trigger / initiating event;
- actor and role;
- required inputs and preconditions;
- object or record created;
- state transitions;
- validations and business rules;
- evidence/documents;
- notifications and escalations;
- approval authority;
- downstream effects;
- reports / outputs;
- exception, rejection, correction and reversal paths.

Create process diagrams or tables where they clarify the workflow. Label inferred steps as inference.

### Phase C — Capability and data extraction

Inspect:
- project/contract hierarchy;
- WBS, work package, BOQ and cost-code relationships;
- contractor/vendor/subcontractor model;
- schedule, progress and measurement;
- procurement, materials and equipment;
- deliverables, submittals and document control;
- QA/QC, inspection, testing and acceptance;
- change orders, claims and commercial controls;
- invoice schedule, billing, retention, payment and tax;
- dashboards, earned value and analytics;
- portals, mobile UX, offline support and collaboration;
- permissions, audit, integrations, APIs and data export;
- AI/automation claims and human approval boundaries.

For each capability capture: source evidence, actor, inputs, outputs, state, authority, integration dependencies, maturity/confidence, and whether it is native, extension, partner or host-platform functionality.

Read relevant current FDG documents before calling anything a gap. At minimum:

Compare each external pattern to existing FDG records and implementations. Classify:
- **REUSE:** existing FDG capability already covers it; cite the exact artifact and evidence.
- **EXTEND:** existing capability needs a bounded enhancement.
- **MERGE:** two or more overlapping capabilities should be consolidated under existing authority.
- **REPLACE:** current approach is demonstrably inferior and a governed replacement case exists.
- **NEW:** a genuinely missing capability with a clear owner and boundary.
- **NO ACTION:** irrelevant, unsupported, uneconomic, unsafe or outside FDG scope.

Do not call something a gap because an external vendor has a feature label that FDG documents differently.

### Phase E — Critical challenge and economics

Challenge:
- What customer pain does this capability solve?
- Is it a must-have, differentiator or marketing label?
- What operational inputs and discipline are needed to make it work?
- What breaks if data is late, incomplete, duplicated or wrong?
- What are the authority, safety, legal and commercial consequences?
- Is it viable for Philippine contractors and subcontractors, including small teams and low-connectivity sites?
- What can be delivered as a small vertical slice?
- What is the lifecycle cost: licenses, setup, integrations, training, support, data migration and lock-in?
- Can FDG provide a simpler, more portable, more auditable or more affordable outcome?

### Phase F — Recommendation and knowledge return

Deliver:
1. product identity and scope;
2. source register with confidence labels;
3. workflow maps;
4. capability matrix;
5. key data objects and relationships;
6. roles, authority and approval states;
7. integrations and technical constraints;
8. source-supported strengths and limitations;
9. FDG comparison with exact wikilinks;
10. REUSE / EXTEND / MERGE / REPLACE / NEW / NO ACTION decisions;
11. prioritized implementation candidates and acceptance tests;
12. risks, unknowns and questions;
13. commercial opportunity and cost/complexity;
14. concise executive recommendation;
15. repository update plan and exact wikilinks.

## 4. Evidence classification

Use these labels consistently:

- **VENDOR CLAIM:** stated by the vendor; not independently verified.
- **DOCUMENTED:** supported by official product documentation or formal marketplace listing.
- **OBSERVED:** directly seen in a product/demo/test; record environment and date.
- **CORROBORATED:** independently supported by credible external evidence.
- **INFERRED:** reasoned conclusion from available evidence; state assumptions.
- **FDG PROPOSAL:** recommendation, not an existing product capability.
- **UNKNOWN:** not established from accessible evidence.

A product feature matrix must include confidence and source references. Absence from public documentation does not prove the product lacks a capability.

## 6. Collaborator allocation

FMCIS coordinator should assign independent perspectives where useful:

- **Product researcher:** source collection, product identity, feature boundaries and source confidence.
- **Domain engineer:** workflow, data model, technical/engineering acceptance and exceptions.
- **Commercial analyst:** pricing, packaging, licensing, customer segments and cost-to-serve.
- **Platform architect:** integrations, deployment, data portability, API and provider dependency.
- **Security reviewer:** permissions, audit, privacy, data residency, external access and threat surface.
- **Independent critic / QA:** challenge unsupported claims, missing workflows, false gaps, duplicated architecture and weak acceptance criteria.
- **Nex:** synthesize findings, reconcile contradictions and maintain architecture coherence.
- **Francis:** final authority for consequential adoption, commercial commitments and architecture changes.

Not every study requires every role. Use the smallest team that provides independent challenge.

## 8. Acceptance criteria

A research package is accepted only when:
- product identity and module boundaries are clear;
- sources are directly inspectable and cited;
- vendor claims are separated from observed/corroborated facts;
- workflows include exception and approval paths;
- FDG existing architecture was inspected before gaps were proposed;
- every proposed gap is linked to exact existing FDG references and evidence;
- each recommendation has a REUSE / EXTEND / MERGE / REPLACE / NEW / NO ACTION disposition;
- copyright/IP boundaries are respected;
- a critic has challenged the report;
- wikilinks resolve to canonical FDG notes;
- source and research dates are recorded;
- a next action is stated without assuming approval.
