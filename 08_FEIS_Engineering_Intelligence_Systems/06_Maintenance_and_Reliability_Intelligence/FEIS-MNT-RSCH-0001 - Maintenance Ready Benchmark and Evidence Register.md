## 3. Why the Benchmark Still Matters

The phrase **Maintenance Ready** highlights a capability gap in FDG's otherwise strong maintenance knowledge:

FDG already captures PM, work orders, parts, manpower, failures, evidence, and reliability.

What was not yet explicit enough was the **readiness layer between identifying work and committing it to execution**, and the **readiness layer between commissioning an asset and expecting operations to maintain it reliably**.

That gap is independently supported by established maintenance-management literature.

## 4. Independent Enhancement Evidence

### SMRP — backlog states and Ready to Schedule

SMRP's "Effective Backlog Management, Part 1" describes maintenance backlog stages including:

- Awaiting Planning
- In Planning
- Awaiting Materials or Services
- Ready to Schedule

and emphasizes that work should not be scheduled before planning, materials, and required services are sufficiently prepared.

Source:
https://smrp.org/Portals/0/Solutions%20issues/SMRP%20Solutions%202017%20September-October.pdf

### SMRP — proactive planner workflow

SMRP community material on the proactive maintenance planner describes a weekly rhythm that includes field inspection, backlog review, parts/material preparation, production/operations coordination, scheduling meetings, and publication of the agreed schedule.

Source:
https://exchange.smrp.org/viewdocument/day-in-the-life-of-a-proactive-main-1

### PEMAC — maintenance management readiness

PEMAC's Maintenance Management Readiness work emphasizes that complex assets often reach operations without the complete maintenance data and systems required to sustain intended value. It specifically links project/design/commissioning information, asset master data, maintenance plans, lifecycle cost, reliability, and operational readiness.

Sources:
- https://www.pemac.org/case-maintenance-management-readiness
- https://www.pemac.org/education/leveraging-asset-master-data-course
- https://www.pemac.org/sites/default/files/Leveraging_Asset_Master_Data_Practitioners_Guide_PEMAC_TMU.pdf

### U.S. DOE — work-package planning and coordination

DOE-STD-1050-93 historically documented the principle that maintenance work-package planning and scheduling should identify technical/administrative requirements and provide materials, tools, and support activities before execution.

The DOE page marks the standard as archived/canceled; FDG uses it as historical good-practice evidence, **not** as a current mandatory code.

Source:
https://www.energy.gov/ehss/articles/doe-std-1050-93

### DOE — O&M best practices

The U.S. Department of Energy O&M Best Practices Guide provides a broader reference for structured operations and maintenance practices, efficiency, cost, and reliability.

Source:
https://www.energy.gov/cmei/femp/articles/operations-and-maintenance-best-practices-guide-achieving-operational-efficiency

## 8. Anti-Pattern Prevention

Do not interpret "maintenance ready" as:

- work order exists;
- technician assigned;
- planned date entered;
- PM due date reached;
- all form fields filled.

Readiness requires removal or explicit acceptance of the constraints that can prevent safe, efficient, quality execution.

Do not conflate:
- Ready to Plan
- Ready to Schedule
- Ready to Execute
- Ready to Return to Service
- Asset Maintenance Ready

Each is a different gate.
